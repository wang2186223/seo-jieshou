Chapter 117

I shut the door. Spun around. Mom was right there, propped against the frame, wearing this totally unreadable expression. We slipped back into the bathroom. She leaned in close, whispering, "How the hell am I supposed to live here for four years and put up with that bastard, knowing what I know now?"

"Honestly? No clue," I said. "But hey, look at the bright side. You’ve got him by the balls now. Use it."

She gave a tight nod, then cranked the faucet off. "I gotta get downstairs. Clean up that mess."

I grabbed her wrist. "Forget it. It can wait. Today was a total shitshow." I glanced out the window. The sky was already turning gray. Dawn. "Seriously. A brutal day." I looked at her again. Dark bags hung under her eyes. "Let's just crash on my new bed. We'll deal with this crap later."

We crawled under the covers. Thank god the delivery guys actually showed up yesterday and we got the fresh sheets on before the whole pizza disaster blew up.

Boom. Two minutes later, we were both dead to the world.

Next thing I know, I’m hauling ass through some weird woods. Creepy, massive trees everywhere. Beautiful, but dark as hell. A strange vibe washed over me—like I’d been here before, maybe? These trunks were freaking huge, bigger than anything I'd ever seen.

I scrambled around, looking for anyone, anything, to tell me where the hell I was. But the ground was pure muck. My shoes kept sinking into the wet mud.

"Where the fuck am I?" I yelled.

"You know exactly where you are, silly girl."

I whipped around. Dad’s grandma was standing right there.

"Grandma?"

"Hey there, pup." She threw an arm around my waist, pulling me along. We trudged through the soggy dirt, heading deeper and deeper into the pitch-black woods.

"Why are we in the middle of nowhere?" I asked.

"This isn't just some random forest," she said. "This is where our bloodline started. Our roots are right here, and there’s something you seriously need to see."

"Like what?"

"It’ll explain your power. And why the wolf goddess actually teamed up with us." She dragged me forward. Thick vines hung down like ropes. The heat was suffocating, sweat pouring down my spine. "Come on. Watch how we began."

We walked a bit more. Suddenly, a scream ripped through the air. Pure, raw agony.

"No! Bring him back!" a woman shrieked, sobbing her eyes out. "Just bring him back!"

"What's happening?" I whispered, leaning into Grandma.

"Don't touch anything. Just watch," she muttered. She yanked me faster. We were practically sprinting over the slick ground now.

I tripped over a massive root and went flying, landing flat on my hands and knees. Grandma knelt right beside me. "Look," she whispered.

A young guy lay there, covered in nasty bites. Definitely wolf teeth. A woman with wild, fiery red hair was hunched over his lifeless body. Dark, thick blood was oozing out slowly. It was depressing as hell.

"Bring him back," she begged. Nothing. Silence. "I’ve worshipped you my entire life!" she screamed, her voice cracking. "When everyone else walked away, I stayed! I was loyal!" She shoved her hands right into the puddle of blood. She smeared the red stuff all over her face, then down her arms. "I’m calling on you, Three-Faced Goddess! Save my boy! Old gods, save my family! We stayed true, we did our duty, we stood strong when the new gods came!" She threw her hands up, defying the sky. "Mother, save my son!"

The wind went crazy, howling around her. Moonlight flooded the stone circle, hitting these jagged rocks stacked like steps. My jaw hit the floor. I looked at Grandma, but she just pressed a finger to her lips. Shh.

"Maiden, I’m begging you, save my son!" the woman yelled, her voice getting louder and louder. "He’s just a kid! Barely even a man!" She started drawing symbols on his skin with the blood—runes I recognized from my own book.

"Mother, save my son! He always worshipped you, he’ll keep our line alive!" She painted his lips red with his own blood. Out of the shadows, a wolf crept closer.

"Crone, save my son! He’ll make sure our family worships you for hundreds of years!" Tears streamed down her face as she screamed at the moon. "Three-Faced Goddess, save my boy!" The wind whipped harder. She kept screaming, arms raised high, his blood dripping down her skin. "Three-Faced Goddess, please! Maiden, I call you! Mother, I call you! Crone, I call you!"

BOOM. Lightning cracked.

Two women stepped out of the dark. One of them had a face that kept shifting—young girl, mother, old hag. The other one had a pack of wolves walking right behind her.
