Chapter 249

The room went ice-cold. Dead silence. Every single eye locked onto those two guys. Nobody dared to blink. We were all just waiting for the car crash to happen.

Like, did this idiot seriously just confess to that? I pressed my fingers to my lips, trying to choke back a laugh. But then Sable shot me this look, and boom—I lost it.

I doubled over, laughing my ass off. Tears were literally streaming down my face. I popped back up, clutching my side because it hurt so bad.

"What the hell is so funny, girl?" Hawthorn spat, glaring.

I wiped my eyes. "Man, you are straight-up stupid." I wiped my face again, shaking my head with a heavy sigh. "You'd think a guy your age would have a brain cell or two, but nope. Here we are." I waved a hand around, did a quick spin to face the crowd, then whipped back to him. "You just admitted to rigging the queen selection. Right in front of the king. The same king you're trying to force into a mating contract when he's waiting for his actual, goddess-given mate. You basically just told the whole room that you guys teamed up behind his back to make him your puppet." My grin vanished. I let my voice drop, cold and sharp. "Honestly, it sounds a lot like you're committing treason. Like you're trying to snatch the crown and steal the king's power for your little council, right to his face." I took a step closer. "But hey, you can't possibly be *that* brain-dead, right?"

"Oh, trust me. He absolutely is that brain-dead." Orion's voice was pure ice.

"My king, wait! That's not what I meant at all!" Hawthorn threw his hands up, totally backing down.

"Then what did you mean? Spell it out for me. Talk to me like I'm as dumb as you think I am." I could literally hear the guy gulp from across the room.

"My king..." He looked around, panicked, before his eyes locked onto me. "As your council, it's our job to make sure your mate is the absolute best. We look at the whole package: rank, family, money, influence. How she fits with us, and how she fits with the pack." He pointed a finger right at me. "Just look at Lyra." I let out a low growl, but he didn't stop. "She's got a nasty temper. And her dad? A total traitor."

That did it. I snapped. I lunged forward and grabbed him right by the throat. "Say that again," I squeezed hard. "I fucking dare you to say it again."

Orion stepped in, wrapping his hand around my wrist. "Let him go." I whipped my head around to look at him. My eyes were burning gold, and I guess he saw the pain in them because he flinched. "Please," he muttered.

I slammed Hawthorn onto the floor. "I'm letting you off the hook for Raven's sake," I said, crouching down next to the wheezing old man. "But just this once. You open your mouth about my mom or dad again, and someone's going to be howling for a funeral tonight." I stood up and stomped off.

Orion hauled Hawthorn back to his feet. "Why the hell did you call Caspian a traitor?" his voice boomed, calling him out in front of everyone.

"My lord, he's the reason your father is dead!"

Orion growled, a deep, angry sound. "No. My father died because the council rerouted his army at the last second, and his own guards sold him out." He got right in Hawthorn's face. "But it's funny. You blamed Caspian when I was just a kid, and I actually believed your lies. But Caspian was back in his own territory, saving his own family and burying his own people. He wasn't anywhere near the royal pack."

I leaned back against the table, crossing my arms. "So what, we're blaming any wolf who protects his own family instead of the king now? Even if he's a thousand miles away? Hell, by that logic, can I blame you for the raid on my dad's pack that got him killed?"

Hawthorn's eye twitched. Just a tiny bit. My stomach instantly knotted. He had something to do with it.

Orion caught my eye and gave a barely visible shake of his head. *Drop it for now.* But he saw it too. I gave him a tiny nod back, and he turned back to the old man.

"That's not fair!" Hawthorn puffed his chest out, crossing his arms like a toddler. "I was nowhere near your father's land."

"Exactly," I said, shaking my head. "So keep his damn name out of your mouth."

"Anyway." Orion shook his head, cutting through the noise. "Let's go back to the part where the council already picked my mate." He glared. "You don't get to choose my mate, Hawthorn. Nobody does. You can suggest names, sure, but I decide if I even want to look at them." Hawthorn's eyes narrowed, but he finally gave a tight nod.

"Fine. Whatever. We're done here. She can keep playing her little application game. The council knows we made the right call anyway. Let's go, girls." He turned to walk out.

"Hold up. I don't think we're done at all." I said, stopping him cold. Everyone whipped around to look at me. I just stared down at my nails, acting bored. "According to the old laws—the ones your own father wrote—any council member has to take an oath. You swear to stay out of power struggles, stay out of pack drama, and give up your rank entirely. And you definitely can't use your aura or commands on unranked wolves without their alpha's permission." I looked around the room. "And guess what? There are no pack alphas here. Which means you just broke your vow." I locked eyes with him, watching the realization hit him like a truck. "And the penalty for breaking that vow? You get kicked off the council. Permanently."
