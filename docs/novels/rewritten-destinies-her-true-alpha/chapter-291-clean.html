Chapter 291

I was just throwing on my clothes when a knock rattled the door. I grabbed a towel, wrapped up my wet hair, and yanked it open. Boom. Juniper. She shoved a glass of red wine right into my face, pushed past me, and kicked the door shut.

"Alright, spill," I said, flopping onto the mattress. I took a gulp. I knew her. She had some heavy shit on her mind and wanted to hash it out with me before the rest of the crew got involved.

"So, I’ve been chewing on what you said..." she started. "About the founding families."

I nodded. "Yeah? What about 'em?"

"What if you’re actually right?" She started pacing, wearing a groove in the floor. "What if our ancestor gods literally rigged the game, shoved us into the same room, and that's why we're here?"

I shrugged, taking another swig. "Okay, and? Does that seriously bug you?"

She whipped around, glaring. "It doesn't bug *you*? We got forced together. No choice, just shoved into each other’s lives, and boom, here we are." She stepped closer. "Like, do we even have free will?"

I stared her down, taking a massive gulp this time. "What, you hate being my friend or something?"

She sucked her teeth, making a sharp *tsk* sound. "Duh, of course not."

I tilted my head. "Are the other girls making you miserable?"

She threw her hands up. "Ugh! No! That’s not it either!"

"Then stop dancing around it and just spit it out!" My temper was flaring. I literally had to bite my tongue to keep from saying something nasty.

She froze. I saw the sheer panic flash in her eyes. She walked over and plopped down next to me on the bed. "What if—"

I cut her off with a flick of my wrist. "Cut the 'what ifs'. Talk to me, Juniper. For real."

She stared at me for a long second, trying to find her voice. Then she looked away, chewing on her bottom lip. "If we were forced into this..." she mumbled, her voice barely a whisper. "Doesn't that, like, ruin everything?"

I watched her. "Ruin it how?"

She snapped her head back to me, anger taking over. "Stop playing dumb! Just answer me!"

I pinched the bridge of my nose. A killer headache was starting, and god, I just wanted to chill. "Ruin *what*, Juniper? Are you saying you'd dump me if you found out some god set us up?" My temper finally snapped. "Are you seriously ready to flush three years of friendship down the toilet just because of how we met?"

She flinched like I’d slapped her. "No! No way!"

"So you think the other girls are gonna throw away everything we built just because you decided to sit at my table one day?"

"Of course not!"

"So it’s me. You think *I'm* gonna walk away because of some cosmic matchmaking. Like you saving my life and taking care of me meant absolutely nothing to me?"

"NO!" she shrieked.

I stood up and marched out. Juniper scrambled right after me. "Where are you going?!"

"I'm going to ask them myself." I headed straight down the stairs. "Hey, girls!"

I burst into the kitchen. Daisy and Fawn were busy prepping dinner. Tears were stinging my eyes, and Juniper was stomping in right behind me. "We need to talk," I said.

"We don't need to do this right now," Juniper muttered, shaking her head.

"Oh, yes we do," I snapped. "You have questions, and now I want answers too." I grabbed the open wine bottle off the counter and topped off my glass. I took a huge gulp, then turned to face the room. "If any of you are overthinking things like Juniper—and let's be real, I know a couple of you are—you might have some thoughts about that whole god conversation we had."

Daisy set her spoon down and leaned over the counter. "Wait, what are you talking about?"

I gestured to Juniper. "Go ahead. Explain. Then I'll ask my question."

Juniper glared at me, but then her shoulders slumped. She caved. "The gods," she sighed, waving her hands around. "If we're all descended from them, and we all just *happened* to become best friends... it's too neat. It feels totally rigged."

"Wait, what?" Luna looked at me, totally lost. "I'm confused."

"She thinks," I said, "that because we're all from the founding families, us being this close is too perfect to be a coincidence. She thinks the gods set us up."

Juniper pointed at me, nodding fast. "Exactly! That!"

Fawn didn't even stop stirring the pot. "Okay... and?"

Juniper gasped, taking a step back. "What do you mean, 'and'?"

"I mean, so what?" Fawn looked up. "Maybe they did nudge us. So? That doesn't mean we didn't choose this. We chose to hang out. We chose to be this tight. Honestly, who cares if some god pushed me toward you guys? You're my best friends. Period."

One by one, the girls nodded, turning their eyes to Juniper.

Juniper just stood there, jaw dropped, staring at us. "I thought... I really thought you guys would throw it all away."
