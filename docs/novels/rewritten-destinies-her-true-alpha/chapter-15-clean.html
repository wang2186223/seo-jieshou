Chapter 15

My old man slammed to a halt. We were at the edge of a tiny clearing. Right in the middle sat a tree stump. I slid off Obsidian’s hairy back. He trotted off, shifted back to his human skin, and walked back over. He pointed at the stump. "Sit."

I nodded. Walked out there. The sun was barely hitting the treetops, leaving the clearing packed with deep, creepy shadows. A shiver ran down my spine. Not from the cold—wolves run hot as hell. It was the woods. Something was out there. Waiting.

I spun back to my dad. He was still hanging back at the tree line. "Why are you staying over there?"

"This isn't my gig. It's yours. I already did my time here."

"Your time? What the hell does that mean?"

"It’s a sacred spot. Our family’s power lives in this clearing. And it’s locked down against it."

"Locked down? Dad, make sense."

"Just shut up and listen. Sit on the stump." I dropped my butt onto the wood. "Now, shoes and socks off. Bare feet on the dirt."

"Why?"

He let out a sharp laugh. "You gonna question every single thing I say?" He shook his head, waving his hands at me.

I ripped off my socks and shoes. Shot him a look. "Happy now?"

"Yeah. Lesson one: get in sync with nature. We suck our power from the earth. Give and take. Push and pull. Action, reaction." Dad planted his boots and took a massive breath. The trees literally leaned toward him, like his lungs were vacuuming them in. Then he blew it out. The trees whipped backward, shivering and dancing in the wind like they were throwing a party. "Sometimes we take power. Sometimes, like right now, I give it back."

My jaw hit the dirt. I looked around, eyes wide. "How the hell did you do that?" I looked from the dancing trees to his face. He was beaming.

"Magic." He laughed. It was infectious. I couldn't help but laugh too. "Now, to pull that off, like I said, step one is connecting."

"How?" My brain was fried. I only knew how to connect to Wi-Fi.

"Focus on the air. Feel the wind. Feel your feet on the grass, the dirt holding you up. Feel the trees sucking juice from the sun and soil. Feel the animals living off the land. It’s all wired together. Once you plug in, you’ll feel everything. It’s gonna blow your mind. You’ll feel... alive. Really alive, for the first time. That’s the only way to put it."

"Fine." I shut my eyes. Felt the wind whisper through the branches. Wiggled my toes in the grass. I felt like an idiot. "How do I know if this is working?" I cracked one eye open. Dad was gone. "Hello? Dad?" I stood up, walking toward the edge. "Dad?" The dark trees shifted. I backed up.

Alone. In the middle of nowhere. Clueless. It was too dark to stumble through the brush, and I felt eyes on me from the shadows. Goosebumps broke out on my arms. I turned away from the dark.

"Dad! Hey!" I yelled. Panic started clawing at my throat. How was I supposed to get back?

*He told you what he could. Focus. He’ll be back in a few hours once you actually try.*

Umbra’s voice made me jump. I’d been too busy freaking out over my dad vanishing. "I don't know what I'm doing!"

*He can't explain it. Obsidian told me the same thing. He said his piece, now it's on us to figure it out.*

I plopped back onto the stump. Closed my eyes. But every snap, every rustle had me twitching.

*Nothing's gonna hurt you here. It's sacred ground. You're safe. Now focus.*

I took a deep breath. Calmed my nerves. Closed my eyes again. Wind, grass, breathing. In. Out. Slowly, the world faded to black.

"Fell asleep, huh?"

I jolted, slipped off the stump, and face-planted. Dad was standing there, laughing his ass off.

"What?"

"It's fine. I passed out my first time too. Come on. Lunchtime." I scrambled up. Dad ruffled my hair. "Let's go, sleeping beauty. I'm starving." He walked off, shifted back into Obsidian. I jumped on his back, and we headed home.

"Move it! Faster! You're wolves, not humans! You can do better than this crap!" I screamed at the top of my lungs. I was standing next to the Beta and Gamma females, Astrid and Freya. They watched the pack girls struggle to jog around the indoor track. I turned to them, eyes wide. "What the hell is this?"

Astrid shook her head, watching the trainwreck. "The girls here only train if they feel like it. Your dad doesn't make us."

"I told him it was a bad idea." Freya, the Gamma, sneered at the girls. "Look at them. Two laps and they're dying." The buff woman shook her head, throwing her hands up. "I don't get this lazy crap. What happens if we get hit?"

"Well, I'm here for two months. After I leave, you two are running this show." They both snapped their eyes to me. I nodded. "I talked to my dad. He folded once I brought up the last pack that kept this outdated, sexist rule."

"Which pack?" Astrid frowned.

"Stonebrooke." I shook my head. "A few survivors got moved by the council before I left."

"What?" Astrid stared back at the girls. Freya’s jaw dropped.

"The pack is wiped out?" Freya asked. I nodded. "Holy crap."

"The second biggest pack went down. Where does that leave us?" Astrid wiped her face, then roared at the track, "Hustle up, ladies!" A massive groan echoed, but I just smirked.

"It leaves us right here, teaching these girls to fight for their lives." I clapped my hands and walked over. "That's enough. Bring it in." The girls limped over. I had to bite back a laugh. They looked pathetic. "Ladies... that was..."

"Tragic," Astrid cut in.

"Pathetic," Freya spat.

"A start." I shook my head at the two beside me and laughed. "It's new. It sucks. But it's non-negotiable."

"Why?" some girl yelled.

"First, it keeps you alive in a raid. Second, once you shift, it locks in your connection with your wolf. And third—the big one for this pack—it proves that outside of ranked wolves, aura power is garbage. A weak-aura wolf can and will slaughter a stronger one if she has a reason to."

Someone snorted. I looked over. Morgana was smirking. I smirked right back. Astrid sneered. "It's true. Rare, but I saw a weak wolf gut a beta to protect her warrior mate during a raid."

"How is that even possible?" a voice called from the back.

"Wild wolves have a hierarchy, but our packs only have a few official ranks. Alphas, betas, gammas, omegas. Everyone else lives in the system but outside the actual power structure." I laid out the basic wolf setup.

"We know all this," Morgana called out, snickering with her clique of mean girls.

"If you knew it, this pack wouldn't be having these damn issues," I shot back. "Are you a ranked wolf, Morgana?"

"I will be," she snapped. I laughed.

"Do you know the odds of an alpha wolf mating with an unranked wolf?" I tilted my head, staring her down. She glared. "Zero. Zero percent. Even if the guy liked the girl, the wolves would never allow it. The female wolf would roll over and show her belly, and the alpha wolf would rip her throat out for even thinking she could make him bow. That’s what an alpha female does. She backs her mate, but she pushes back. She stands up to him. A weak wolf can't do that." I locked eyes with Morgana until she finally looked away.

She needed to get it through her thick skull that she was never going to end up with my dad.

"What 'issues' are you talking about?" one of her little friends piped up, breaking the ice.

"You've got unranked wolves acting like they run the place. Using their power to bully, demean, and boss other wolves around."

"So?"

My temper flared at her smug tone. "So, what if I ordered you to do jumping jacks until your heart exploded?"

She flinched, stammering. "You... you can't do that."

"Who's gonna stop me?"

Her eyes darted around, begging for backup. No one said a word. "The Council."

"The Council also says unranked wolves can't throw their weight around weaker ones," I snapped back. "So what, the Council is only useful when it saves your ass, but you don't have to follow their rules?"

The silence was deafening.
