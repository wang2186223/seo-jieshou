Chapter 106

I nudged the glass patio door with my snout, looking back at Mom. She got the hint, rushed over, and threw it open. I stepped out, leaving that trainwreck of a room behind. Let those three idiots clean up my mess. "Mom," I barked. She scrambled right out after me.

She caught up, falling into step beside me. "Jesus, Umbra... did you really have to go that far?"

"Hell yeah, I did," I snapped. "She pushed it. We're done putting up with her crap." I looked her dead in the eye. "Shift. Let's run." A tiny smirk tugged at her lips. She nodded and went full wolf in a flash. Her brown coat gleamed. She was slightly smaller than Umbra, sure, but still way bigger than any normal wolf.

I bolted, and she stayed right on my tail. The moment we hit the tree line, I tapped the brakes. This was her turf, not mine, so I let her take the lead. She blasted past me, weaving through thick brush and tight trees like it was nothing. Me? I was barely dodging the branches.

I tripped over a massive root, and she let out a mocking snort. I scrambled back up and drew level with her. "Hey! We need to talk!" I yelled. She gave a quick nod and accelerated, turning into a brown blur. Man, she looked exactly like Terra in my head right then.

We hit a creek. I figured she'd stop, but nope—she leaped straight in and swam. I dove after her. Holy crap, it was freezing. I tried to howl but ended up swallowing half the creek. By the time I dragged myself to the bank, she was literally rolling on the grass, laughing her ass off. I glared at her. "You're a total klutz," she wheezed. Okay, fair point. I was still getting used to this new skin.

"Throwing hands was way easier than this," I grumbled.

"That was pure survival mode," she said, nudging me back onto my feet. "You had to fight, so your body just did it. This? This is just basic coordination. Give it time. Come on, kiddo, we've got ground to cover." With that, she vanished into the deep brush. I trotted after her, keeping a slower pace.

We pushed hard for two hours until a stone cabin popped up in a clearing. "What is this place?" I asked, tilting my head. Mom shifted back to her human self. Wait, hold on. She still had her clothes on. "Whoa. How did you do that? Where'd the clothes come from?"

She threw a grin over her shoulder and cracked the door. "Get inside, I'll tell you." I shifted back, too, and—holy hell—I was fully clothed. "How the hell is this happening?" I gasped.

"Seriously? You never noticed?" She waved me inside, and I stepped through the door.

"My first shift was a bloodbath, and then I woke up in a hospital bed," I muttered. "I don't remember the shift back." Mom nodded, walking further into the dusty room. She ripped the white sheets off the furniture as she passed, tossed them in a heap by the hearth, and knelt down. She shoved some dry kindling under the pre-stacked logs and sparked it up.

The wood caught, and that sweet, smoky smell filled the dark cabin. "You gonna stand there like a statue, or are you coming in?" Mom asked, turning around. She looked totally at peace here. I shut the door behind me, plunging the room into near-total darkness since the windows were completely blocked. I shuffled closer to the fire just to get a decent look at her.

"Seriously, Mom, where are we?"

"Hold your horses," she said. "Let me get set up, then we'll talk." She started pacing around, bringing the room to life. She yanked down the wooden shutters and lit a few old oil lamps. I watched, totally lost, as she wandered into what looked like a makeshift kitchen and grabbed some gear.

She came back with her hands full, dumping a kettle, some mugs, and jars onto the hearth. Then she slipped outside. I heard water splashing, and a second later, she was back inside, shutting the door. "Where. Are. We?" I demanded, dropping my voice an octave. She just smirked.

"We're home," she said. "Well, my home, anyway." She did a slow little spin, beaming, then walked back to the hearth. She squatted down and hung the kettle on a hidden hook over the flames. "I lived here before I ever met your dad. My mom raised me right here, hidden away from my dad's pack, until I grew up."

"Why?" I asked.

"Because..." She looked up, locking eyes with me. "We're the last of the Lycans."
