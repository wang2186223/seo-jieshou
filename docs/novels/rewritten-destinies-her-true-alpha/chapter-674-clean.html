Chapter 639

I spun around to face my grandma. "Alright, what's the play here?"

Rhea just chuckled. "Look at you. All that raw power, and you're still clueless." She flicked her wrist. Boom. The dirt right under my palm scooped itself out, hardening into a stone bowl. Out of nowhere, water bubbled up, filling it to the brim. "The earth can't bail you out this time, kiddo." She took a step in, her eyes dead serious but kind. "Do what I do. Summon them. You've got the dirt, the sky, and the ocean in your veins. Reach out."

I went for the obvious first. I shoved my hand right into the bowl, letting the cold water wrap around my skin. But damn, it felt dead. Not like the dirt. No spark, no pulse, nothing. I looked up at the goddess and shook my head. "Yeah, I got nothing. It's not clicking like it did with you and the ground."

She smirked. "You're thinking way too small, kid. Close your eyes." I did what she said. "Water is life, just like dirt," she whispered. "Sure, the earth grows food, but without water? You get zero. No humans, no plants, not even microscopic bugs. It's the planet's lifeblood. My blood. It pumps through me, feeding the soil, keeping the whole world breathing. Now, listen. Hear the ocean. Hear the things swimming in it." Boom. Suddenly, waves were slamming against rocks inside my head. I heard the deep groan of whales and the sharp clicks of dolphins. "Now call him. My boy, Neptune. If you've got the guts... he'll show."

I let myself drown in that wet sensation. Sure, my knees were still dug into the dirt, but my mind was gone. The ocean current yanked me off the land, dragging me down into a pitch-black, bone-crushing abyss. "Neptune," I muttered. The water shivered. "Neptune! Get out here!" A massive shadow started taking shape in the blackness.

A giant sea serpent—had to be a mile long—shot straight at me. Its eyes were pure black ocean, its teeth like razor blades. "Who's making noise?" A heavy, dark voice rattled my chest, making me choke for air. The snake lunged, jaws wide, ready to swallow me whole. Right before those fangs snapped shut, a boot crunched on grass nearby. "Hey, Ma." The voice was still deep, but warm now. My eyes snapped open.

Standing over me was a mountain of a man, easily bigger than Orion. His skin was pale, almost see-through, and he had those same pitch-black snake eyes. He leaned down, hooked a finger under my chin, and tilted my face up. "Well, aren't you a cute little thing?" He let out a slow grin, showing off a row of terrifyingly sharp fangs.

"You actually came," I whispered.

His grin stretched wider, flaunting those fangs. "Hey, you called."

Rhea grabbed his shoulder and yanked him back, scowling. "Back off, Neptune. She's got to save her pack from the scourge." She sneered, and Neptune instantly froze. He tilted his head, studying me. "You're not human, are you?"

"Closer than you," I muttered, shaking my head. "But no."

He gave a quick nod. "Then you're gonna need my brother." I nodded back. "Go ahead, call him. But good luck, the guy's a total flake." He chuckled at his own joke, then stepped back to stand by his mom.

I pulled my hand out of the bowl, letting the breeze dry my skin as I shut my eyes. If Rhea was the flesh and Neptune was the blood, then Aether had to be the breath in our lungs. It keeps everything alive, feeds our cells, and keeps us going just like water. I locked onto the air filling my chest. Instantly, the wind started whipping wild circles around the clearing.

It started as a breeze, but I remembered yesterday. I remembered how terrifyingly loud it got right before I ripped lightning from the clouds. Wind can tear roofs off houses and push oceans aside, but it can also soothe your soul. My hair lashed across my face. Far off, eagles screamed. The wind roared louder and louder, sounding like a stampede of wild horses charging straight at me. "Aether!" I yelled into the gale. "Aether, get down here!" I screamed over the howling storm.

Then came the heavy, rhythmic thumping of massive wings. In my mind's eye, I saw it—a giant thunderbird circling overhead. Lightning flashed, the same bolts I'd summoned before, and thunder boomed in my ears. The massive bird stared right at me. "Who the hell are you?" Its wings beat so hard they drowned out the storm, the sheer pressure popping my ears with every flap.

"Nobody special! Just trying to save my people!" I yelled back. The bird suddenly dove straight at me. I braced myself, waiting for the impact, but instead, a warm hand gently cupped my cheek.

"Who told you you're not important, little wolf?"
