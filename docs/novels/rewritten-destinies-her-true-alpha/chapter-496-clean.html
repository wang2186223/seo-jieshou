Chapter 496

I snorted. A laugh slipped out because, honestly? That was pure bullshit. If Roric was my granddaddy, I’d damn well know it. My old man never shut up about the guy. "Oh, come on," I muttered.

My dad just shook his head, staring at me like I’d lost my mind. "You’re positive you heard that name? Roric?"

"Dead positive," I said, nodding. I yanked my phone back out. "Gideon Stone dug this up." I shoved the screen right under his nose. He tapped the email, his eyes darting back and forth, scanning the text at warp speed.

"No way. This is a joke," my dad muttered under his breath. "The King killed my father. He's dead."

Cipher shook his head. "Wrong. He didn't."

My dad’s head snapped up, eyes locking onto him. "The hell did you just say?"

Cipher let out a heavy sigh. "Look, the old king had already set up the council by the time your dad started raising hell. Sure, he got a death sentence, but carrying it out? That was the council's job."

I scanned the room. Man, there were a lot of sketchy-looking faces in here. I didn't trust half of them. *Umbra, go find Obsidian,* I linked. *Ask him if it's cool if I use magic in front of this crowd.* Umbra took off like a shot while my dad kept staring blankly at my phone screen.

"The council?" Juniper’s dad turned to Cipher, who just gave a grim nod. "They're popping up everywhere lately, aren't they?"

Umbra zipped back in. *Obsidian says go for it. He's thrown magic around in front of these guys before.*

I stood up, squaring my shoulders. "Grandma. Get over here. I need you, and I've got questions."

The room went dead silent. Everyone stopped talking and stared straight at me. "What is she talking about?" someone muttered in the back.

Then, a soft "Oh" echoed behind me. I spun around instantly.

"Grandma," I said, facing her. She looked around the crowded room, then her eyes locked onto mine.

"Well, look at that," she sighed. "Judging by the look on your dad's face, I guess you've got a ton of stuff to ask me." She let out another sigh.

"Who the fuck is that?" one of the Alphas whispered.

I laughed. I mean, what else was I supposed to do?

I turned to face the seated men. "Look, you all know my dad has magic. And yeah, it comes from his bloodline. I'm guessing you guys know that much. His mom—my real grandmother—was a daughter of the first family. The first line of wolves. But here's the part you didn't know: the very first wolf was actually a witch's son. A crazy powerful bloodline that settled this area way before anyone else showed up. The three-faced goddess couldn't save her own kid, so the moon goddess stepped in. But his dad... Roric? He's the son of a witch who fell hard for a wolf." I pointed right at my grandmother. "And this is his mother."

"The witch?" someone gasped.

My grandmother flashed a smile. "Yep, that's me." She reached out, her hand hovering to cup my cheek. "How are you doing, sweetheart?"

"Totally confused," I smiled back, raising my hand to meet hers. I couldn't actually feel her touch, but I closed my eyes and pretended I could.

"That's fine, honey. Being confused just means you're missing some pieces of the puzzle." Her voice was pure silk. I nodded.

"Exactly." I snapped my eyes open.

"Roric..."

I watched her eyes flash as she took a deep, heavy breath. Then she turned to my dad. "I wanted a much better life for you, my boy."

My dad swallowed hard, trying to clear the lump in his throat. "But... he's dead." He said it, but his voice hitched at the end, making it sound like a question.

My grandmother shook her head. "No. He's very much alive."

My dad slumped, the wind completely knocked out of him. "How?" he whispered.

My grandmother walked over, reaching out to rest a hand on his shoulder. "You know how. He's got magic, just as much as you do, even if he spent his whole life denying it."

The room started whispering.

"So the bastard used magic to break out," Cipher growled, his voice low and dangerous.

She nodded. "Yep. He's been pulling everyone else's strings like they're puppets. He was a fool back then, and he's still a fool now." She turned her gaze to me. "He hates you. He hates your dad. But honestly? He's saving most of his rage for you."

My dad bolted upright. "Why?"

"Because she's a woman, and she's stronger than him. Smarter, too. He absolutely loathes anyone who outshines him." She shook her head, looking sad. "I raised my boy to be kind, loving, and forgiving. But the second his wolf kicked in, he snapped. He got completely obsessed with the first family. With the king. With the lycans."

Cipher snorted. "So all this drama is just because he wasn't a Lycan?"

She shook her head. "I don't know why he went bad. This pack always accepted him. They cared for him. But his obsession turned toxic. And I knew we couldn't let him know the truth."

"What truth?" Kael demanded.

My dad tilted his head, realization dawning on him. "That's why my mother ordered me to never tell him we were the first line."

My grandmother nodded. "He never loved her. He just lusted after the power of this pack. He was so busy hunting for the first line, trying to trace the power back to the goddess. He never guessed the very thing he wanted was right under his nose. Your mother's father made damn sure of that."

The goddess shook her head. "You saved yourself." She leaned down and pressed a kiss to his forehead. Then she turned to the rest of the room. "You are all devoted to my path, or the path of my sisters and brothers." She looked straight at Juniper's dad. "Your daughter did good. She'll get her reward—if she makes it through the final battle."

He swallowed hard, instantly on high alert. "The battle?"

The goddess gave him a sad, faint smile. "I don't see the future. I just lay down the path."

Cipher shifted his weight, and she spun to face him. "Hiding behind a mask won't change a damn thing."

His head snapped up. "Me?" He sounded totally lost.

She chuckled. "Well, who else is wearing a mask around here?"

Cipher scratched his head. "Uh, nobody."

"Exactly." She walked over and leaned in close. "You're hiding to protect yourself. But you're only locking yourself out of what could be. You're keeping your head down, and I'm worried you're gonna miss your shot. If you don't spill the truth—all of it—you're gonna lose the one thing you want most in this world."

Cipher swallowed hard. "But what if I screw up again?"

She shrugged a shoulder. "Then you screw up. But holding back isn't doing you any favors."

She turned away. "Stay on the path, or don't. It's up to you guys."

And just like that, I was back. I stumbled, losing my footing for a second, but Cipher caught me and held me steady.

"You good?" he asked softly.

"Yeah." I patted his hand, and a sudden spark zapped between our skin. I frowned, pulling my hand back, then turned to face the others. "So, I guess you guys have some questions." I tried to force a smile, but it felt totally fake.

Everyone was just staring at me, wide-eyed.

"That... that was the goddess," Rhys said, his face completely pale.

I nodded. "Yeah. It was."

"Could that have been some kind of magic trick?" Kael looked around, but the others just shrugged, clueless.

I let out a long breath. "Magic can do a lot of crazy things." I smiled. "I can change my face. I can change my voice. But there's one big rule you don't break."

My dad nodded. "It's forbidden to fake a celestial."

"Exactly," I said. "If anyone tries to play goddess, they lose their blessings instantly. Magic, shifting, whatever gifts they had. Rank, power, aura—all of it gets stripped away on the spot."

Juniper's dad shook his head. "No way in hell was that fake." He looked around at the other alphas. "I've felt the moon goddess before. I know you guys have too." The others nodded. "Look me in the eye and tell me that wasn't her."

Ash looked away, avoiding his gaze. "Okay, fine, she felt like the goddess. I was just trying to look at all the options."

I nodded. "Fair enough. But let me ask you something." I tilted my head, locking eyes with him.

He met my stare and nodded. "Go ahead."

"Ever since you walked into this office, you and a couple of others have been acting like you're against my dad."

The whole room went dead silent, everyone's eyes snapping to me.

"He saved your pack from getting wiped out. He gave you a place to live. He took care of you, trusted you, and treated you with the respect an alpha deserves." Kael opened his mouth to speak, but I slapped a finger up to shut him down. "And when another attack came to kill us all, he brought you to our sacred meeting place. A place no outsider has ever stepped foot in. And you're still doubting him?" I glared at the man, my eyes narrowing. "Why?"

The question hung heavy in the air. Kael looked around at the others, his eyes darting back and forth. "Look, it's not like that," he finally mumbled.

Cipher stepped up, standing right behind me to show he had my back. "Actually, it is exactly like that." He scanned the room. "I've met with you guys plenty of times, and every single time, I see some pushback. Whenever Caspian leaves the room, the whispering starts. I kept my mouth shut because Caspian asked me to, but Lyra's right. After everything he's done for you and your packs, you still act like this? Why?"

Juniper's dad stood up and whipped around to face the others. "I kept quiet to keep the peace. But the Macleans have been nothing but saviors to my pack. My wolves look up to them." He turned to my dad and bowed his head. "I should've spoken up sooner."

"You've done plenty, Conall," my dad said, his voice sounding a bit exhausted.

"I've talked to you about it, yeah, but I should've laid it out for these guys a long time ago." He spun back to the men. "You keep whining about Caspian stealing our power and our packs, but he's never once tried to do that. You complain about being weak alphas. Well, you're right. We are weak. But that's not Caspian's fault."

I stepped over to Juniper's dad. "You're not weak." He looked at me, his eyes softening. I shook my head. "Never call yourselves weak alphas. You're just new. Your packs are small right now, but we're going to help you get strong."
