Chapter 27

I hauled myself up the steps. Finally, I shoved through the tunnel exit and hit a shallow cave. It was carved right into a sheer cliff face. Wait, what? My dad's pack territory didn't have any damn cliffs.

"Where the hell are we?" I muttered. I swept my flashlight beam across the cold stone and scrambled toward the opening. I squeezed out, and holy crap—I was standing right on the edge of the ocean. Waves slammed against the rocks way down below. Off to the left, some rough steps were carved straight into the rock, heading up. I started climbing. I took it slow, real careful, cold mist stinging my face. Suddenly, my boot hit some slimy moss. "Whoa!" I shrieked, arms flailing like crazy to keep from falling. I grabbed the rock, hauled myself over the edge, and gasped. Right there, in a small clearing, sat a tiny cottage looking straight up at the sky.

It looked gorgeous. "No way," I breathed. "This is impossible."

*Only if you think magic is just some fairy tale,* Terra's voice echoed in my head. I gave a quick nod. We trotted down the short dirt path. I shoved the front door open, and bam—my brain glitched. It was like my left eye was seeing one world and my right eye was seeing another.

On one side, the place was flawless. Totally untouched. I could walk right in and see every chair, every table, even the tiny dust specks floating in the air. But on the flip side, the cabin was just a charred, ruined skeleton. The protective runes were shredded, pitch-black, and falling apart. I stepped through the creaking wooden doorway, kicking up a nasty cloud of ash and soot.

This double-vision crap was giving me a killer headache. "What is happening? How?" I stumbled through the living room. My boots squeaked on clean hardwood, but at the exact same time, I was tripping over loose rocks. "Seriously, if anyone says the word 'magic' again, I'm gonna lose my mind and scream." Two chuckles echoed in my head. I smacked the side of my head with my palm. Boom. The clean, cozy cabin vanished. Only the burnt-out wreck remained.

*Well, for that, you'll have to ask the lady who sent you,* Umbra chimed in. *She's the only one with answers.* The air in front of me shimmered, and suddenly, the cozy, comfy living room snapped back. But this time, I heard real, live laughter.

"Come here, sweetie! Where'd you run off to? Grandma's got you!" A little boy with dark hair, bright blue eyes, and two missing front teeth came bursting out of the kitchen. Right behind him walked an absolutely gorgeous woman.

Her face didn't have a single wrinkle, but her hair was streaked with gray. It was like looking at myself a few centuries down the road. She locked eyes with me and went totally stiff. The kid scrambled up the stairs, but she stepped closer. She reached out and gently cupped my cheek. "How?" I whispered.

"Places keep memories," she said, her voice shaking. "You are gorgeous, sweetie. I'm so sorry I can't be there to hug you in real life, but please know I love you with every single piece of my soul."

"You... you know who I am?"

"Duh, you think I wouldn't know my own baby? Kids from my bloodline carry my spark. Like knows like." She leaned closer. "You've had a rough ride, kiddo. But anything can be rebuilt stronger. Roots can dig deeper. Life changes, and we grow."

"But your house... it's..."

"Destroyed, but not really. What you're seeing is just a memory scorched into the dirt I poured my blood and sweat into. The broken runes, the empty shell—that's the real world. Just a sad reminder of the love we had here."

"What happened? And how the hell am I standing on an ocean cliff hundreds of miles away from our pack house?"

"I was never a pack person. I liked my peace and quiet. But hey, magic can bend space itself, linking two spots together. I got my privacy, but I could still see my family." She turned around, dragging her fingers across the table. The furniture flickered and crumbled into ash behind her. She spun around, kicking up a cloud of dust.

I covered my mouth to breathe. "So, what went down here?"

"It was the night of the raid. I felt rogue wolves cross our borders, and I ran to save them. I fought like hell to keep this place hidden, but when I went down, my shields collapsed. Someone found my tunnel." She walked over and sat by the hearth. Instantly, the cabin snapped back to life—a fire roared, and kids' laughter drifted down the stairs. "I had to watch them trash my home. While your dad was busy saving the pack, they burned my place to the ground."

I saw a tear roll down her cheek. "I'm so sorry."

"Don't sweat it, sweetie. They didn't find what they wanted. The exact thing *you're* looking for. Now come on, we're running out of time and you've got a ton to learn." She waved me over and tapped a loose stone. I pressed my hand against it. Flash! The stone vanished, and a book was sitting right there. "Grab it, quick! Someone is hunting you down, and my block won't hold him off much longer. You gotta protect yourself."

Then, just like that, she was gone. I was all alone again, and the cabin that used to be so full of love was nothing but a rotting, broken rune.
