NovelVibe

Chapter 373

Mom shoved us all inside. We crashed in the living room. She did the whole "who wants a drink?" routine, then locked eyes with me. "Hey, sweetie? Why don't you show everyone around? I'm sure Lyra Bloom wants to see your room. You still got that tiny closet packed with your old teddy bears, right?" She flashed a quick grin. "I gotta finish up in the kitchen. Dinner's ready when you guys get back down." Boom. She vanished around the corner. I spun around.

"Alright, tour time," I said. I dragged them around the first floor, but the kid, Lyra Bloom, was clearly over it. She was fidgeting.

"Lyra?" she mumbled.

I turned. "What's up, kiddo?" I dropped to my knees as she shuffled over.

"Your place is cool..."

"But?" I gave her a wink.

"Can we check out your room?" She looked so damn hopeful.

"You bet." I glanced up at Gideon. "It's upstairs." He got the hint and nodded.

"Up we go, baby girl." He scooped her up in one motion. "Stairs are still tough on those little legs, huh?"

She nodded. "Yeah, I get tired fast."

"Same here," I joked, winking again. We headed up. I pointed out a couple of doors, then stopped at mine. "And here's the spot."

I threw the door open. The kid bolted inside. She spun in circles, making me laugh. "This is seriously just your room?" She dashed into the bathroom, did another spin. "Whoa." I waved the adults inside and clicked the door shut. I walked over to the small closet by my mattress and yanked it open. The kid literally shrieked. Stuffed animals. Everywhere. I'd hoarded every single one from my childhood. Dad used to buy me one every single time I went away, until I hit sixteen and told him to cut it out. "Oh my god!" she squealed, running over to grab them. I snickered, then jerked my chin toward the walk-in closet across the room. "Alright, she's busy. We can talk now."

Mara whipped around to face me. "Gideon's keeping me in the dark. He won't say a word at home because of Magnus. But I know damn well something's up, and I want answers. Now."

I nodded. "I'm breaking you guys out," I said, dropping the bomb. "And before you start about how you can't leave because of the kid's Withering—she doesn't have it." I kept my voice down, praying the kid didn't hear.

Mara flinched like I'd slapped her. "What the hell are you talking about?" She glared at Gideon. "Is she out of her mind?"

Gideon rubbed his temples. "Babe, just listen. We grabbed lunch to get Elara and Rhys away from Damon. Lyra stood up for Tern, so Damon beat the crap out of him and fired him. At lunch, I filled her in on Lyra Bloom. Just the basics—why we're here, why I'm working for Magnus."

"Fine, but that doesn't explain her crazy claim," Mara snapped.

"I get it, I get it," Gideon said, pulling out a napkin. "But Lyra has never laid eyes on our kid until today. And Rhys has never mentioned her. Not once."

"Seriously, we haven't," Elara chimed in, stepping closer. "We wanted to give you guys space."

Mara looked from one face to another. "Okay, so she's never seen her. What's your point? Our daughter is dying, and this girl is claiming she's fine, Gideon!"

"Because she *is* fine," I said, checking on the kid. "You guys know anything about witchcraft?"

Mara looked completely blank. "What the hell are you on about?"

Gideon shoved the napkin into her hands. "I told her about the disease. But Lyra asked how old our kid is. She's too old to catch the Withering. It's a curse, Mara. A spell that leaves a specific mark on the wolf. That's what she drew for me." He pointed at the paper in her fist.

"It's a fucking napkin, Gideon! What does a napkin have to do with our little girl?" Mara's voice started rising.

Gideon grabbed her face and shut her up with a hard kiss. "Babe, I love how fierce you get for our kid. But just look at the damn napkin. You'll see." He guided her hand up.

Mara rolled her eyes but flattened the crumpled paper anyway. She froze. Her eyes snapped to mine, wide with shock. "Have you ever seen my daughter before?"

"Never," I said, holding her gaze.

"Any of the kids at her school?"

"Nope," I said honestly.

Her shoulders slumped. "Please," she whispered, her voice cracking. "Don't play games with us. If you can't actually fix this, don't give us hope. I can't take another heartbreak."

I frowned. "What do you mean?"

"She means," a small voice piped up from the corner, "she can't stand hearing another doctor say they can cure me, only to tell me I've got less than a year left."
