NovelVibe

Chapter 294

She went wild, fighting the ropes, the chair, and my magic. I didn't budge. I pinned her down, waiting for that glowing line to lock our circle shut. Boom. Suddenly, fresh lines shot out, crisscrossing between all of us. Everyone got linked to everyone else. It was like this crazy, glowing gold web—a badass, shiny map of how much we loved and backed each other up.

Except it wasn't perfect. Black, nasty rot was eating away at the links. I stood up and scanned the web. Most of the lines were still blazing bright. Hell, all of them were—except for the ones hooked directly to me.

"Everything's solid except your lines," Daisy said, running her fingers along the threads. She gave the cord between us a sharp pluck. A jolt hit my chest, but damn, it felt weak. That black spot right in the middle was spreading fast.

"The louder those damn whispers get, the darker this thing turns," Luna muttered. She flicked her own line. It buzzed, but barely. It was hanging by a thread, ready to snap. Fawn's and Toya's lines looked just as pathetic. Then Luna yanked the cord connected to Juniper. Juniper shrieked. "How the hell do we fix this?" Luna asked.

Daisy locked eyes with me. I shut mine, locking my focus onto the magic. "How could you do this? We're supposed to be your sisters!" Juniper spat, baring her teeth. That hit me like a physical punch to the gut. She didn't count me as family anymore.

Daisy choked back a sob. "We're doing this because you're our sister, idiot." She whipped her head toward me. "Fix it. Now."

She was begging. Luna nodded, pointing at the link. "It's rotting out." I watched the blackness creep further along our connection. Fawn glared daggers at me, and her own line faded even more.

"You're toxic," Fawn snapped, staring at the dying threads. "Just look at that." She pointed at the line between me and Toya. "You trash everything you lay your hands on."

That stung way worse than it should have. But hey, my bad. I was actually letting her words get to me, even though I knew she wasn't in her right mind. But what was pulling her strings? I dropped into the seat next to Juniper and leaned close. "My bad, sis. Forgive me."

She snapped her jaws, trying to take a chunk out of me. Perfect. I used that raw rage as a backdoor. Anger is always an open window to the soul. I shot my magic straight down our line, jumped right over the dead, blacked-out center, and plunged into her head. I let my power worm through her brain, hijacking her own magic to drag myself inside.

One second I'm staring at her foaming mouth, and the next, boom—I'm standing in some weird-ass forest. The trees smelled sweet, but I didn't recognize them at all. Sand and patchy, coarse grass choked the spaces between the trunks. "Where the hell am I?" I muttered.

"My home," a voice said. Sarabi, Juniper's wolf, trotted out from the brush. She fell into step right beside me, walking in silence. "You here to get us out of this mess?"

I halted, turning to look her dead in the eye. "Talk to me. What's going on?"

She avoided my gaze. "See for yourself." She spun on her heel and took off through the woods, heading straight toward whatever was chewing up our link. "Did she mess you up?" she asked, pausing to look back before moving again.

"A bit. But honestly? You've beaten me up worse in practice," I let out a dry laugh.

"Only because you let us, and we weren't actually trying to murder you," Sarabi countered, stopping. "This time, she was. For real." She sat on her haunches, waiting for me to catch up. "I tried to shut her down, but I can't get near her." She looked at me, her eyes full of regret. "I'm locked out."

"What do you mean, locked out?"

"You'll see in a second." She jerked her chin forward, and we walked a few more paces.

The trees suddenly gave way to sand. Pure, golden sand. The sky was a blinding blue, the sun beating down hard. Instantly, I was roasting. Sweat started dripping down my face. "Ugh, why is it like an oven in here?"

"It's where we're from. Our kind of weather. Home," Sarabi said with a dry chuckle. "Juniper is out there. But I'm stuck here." She raised a paw and pushed forward, but it hit thin air and stopped flat, like she was pressing against a solid glass wall. "She locked me out."

"Juniper did?" I stared at Sarabi, but she shook her head.

"No. The other one." She looked at me, her eyes begging. "Save my human. She's my soulmate. I'm not partnering up with anyone else." Partnering up? What the hell did that mean? She caught my blank stare and sighed. "We get a choice. Wolves, I mean. We choose who we share our souls with." She turned her back on me. "Just save her." And then, poof—she was gone.
