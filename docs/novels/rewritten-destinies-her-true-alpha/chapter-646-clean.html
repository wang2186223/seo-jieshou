Chapter 611

**Trigger Warning:** This gets ugly. Physical, mental, and sexual abuse. Skip to Chapter 614 if you can't stomach this.

I gave her hand a hard squeeze. "Spell it out for me."

She sucked in a sharp breath. "My wolf snapped the link with his. I cut the bond myself so I wouldn't feel the full blow of his betrayal. It still stung, sure. Ate at me 24/7. But, damn, I managed." She stared at the ceiling and let out a dry, bitter laugh. "Look at me, dancing around the ugly truth. Like stalling will change what happened. It won't."

"Take your time," I whispered.

She shook her head. "Week one went by, and then Gareth showed up daily." She refused to look at me. "First, he strapped me to a chair so I couldn't fight back. Then the beatings started. But then came the blades. He rubbed wolfsbane into the cuts. Then silver. Every single visit. If I didn't scream or beg the way he wanted, he just cranked up the pain."

My breath hitched. She flinched, trying to yank her hand back, but I held on tight.

"I tried to fake it. Play along. But he saw right through me and made it twice as bad." Her eyes shut tight. "Then, he flipped the script."

"Flipped the script? How?" I frowned.

She nodded, eyes glued to the wall, chest shaking. "He eavesdropped on Magnus and Damon. They were talking about you. About that dumbass Alpha myth."

"What myth?"

She finally locked eyes with me, a single tear tracking down her cheek. "The old legend. An Alpha can't unlock his true power unless he beds his fated mate. The one Celestia picked."

"Okay... but where do you and Gareth fit into that?"

She snorted. "We don't. He's just a moron. He figured if he forced a mating with me, he'd get a power boost." She squeezed her eyes shut, crying harder. "He actually tried to play nice after that. But he was already poison to me. He tried every trick to win me back. God knows why he thought that would work."

"He literally tortured you," I stammered, totally lost. "How does a guy break your bones and then expect you to fall for him?"

Her free fist slammed the table. *Crack.* "Exactly!" she yelled, then slumped, all the fire leaving her. "He was dumb, but he wouldn't quit. And when I kept spitting in his face... well. He decided to just take what he wanted." She looked away, choking back a sob.

"Wait, what? He took your... love?" My brain was fried. I couldn't think straight. She growled, a low rumble, but I gripped her hand harder. "Look, I'm not trying to grill you. I'm just running on empty. I've been running, fighting, dodging death for forty-eight hours straight. My head is spinning. I don't get it."

The growl died. "He strapped me to a table. And he took my innocence. The one thing I saved for my real mate."

The penny dropped. I gasped, my stomach turning. "He... he forced you to—"

"Don't say the word," she rasped, barely audible. "If you say it out loud, I'm right back in that room. And I can't handle that. Not again."

"Hey," I said, squeezing her hand. "Look at me. You're safe. You're here. And that bastard is dead."

She gave a weak nod. Her muscles loosened a fraction. "He kept doing it. Over and over, multiple times a day. Said he was going to suck the power right out of me." She shivered. "Then Magnus walked through the door."

My stomach did a freefall. I knew the punchline, and I hated it. "Please," I whispered, praying to Celestia. "Please tell me you're not about to say what I think you are." But God wasn't listening today.

"He just stood there and laughed. Watched Gareth use me like a piece of meat." She shook, crying silently, tears pouring down. Then she locked eyes with me. "First few times, he just spectated. Funny how he always showed up right when Gareth did. Claimed it was 'pack business.' Yeah, right. Gareth loved having a crowd. But Magnus wanted a piece of the action." She shook her head, disgusted. "The next day, they walked in together. I knew right then." She dry-heaved. "Every day after that, they teamed up. Sometimes twice a day. And then... Ronnie joined." She shuddered hard. Under her skin, patches of fur rippled. Her wolf was trying to soothe her.

"No way," I breathed.

She nodded. "The three of them. They loved every second of breaking me." She rubbed her face, pulling her hand from mine to wipe the tears with both palms. "I broke. I completely lost my mind."

"Anyone would have," I said. My heart ached. But what the hell do you say to someone who survived living hell?

"No, you don't get it." She stared at me, dead-eyed. "I was one inch away from going completely rabid."
