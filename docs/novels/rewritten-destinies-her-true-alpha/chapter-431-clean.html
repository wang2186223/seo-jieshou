Chapter 431

"Right, okay." I let out a heavy sigh. "So maybe I did catch his scent back then, and my brain just, like, wiped the memory." I nodded, trying to sell it to myself. "Yeah. That's gotta be it."

Mom cocked her head, her eyes drilling into me. "What the hell are you rambling about?"

I thudded the back of my head against the drywall. Once. Twice. Then I let out a miserable groan. "Your husband just dropped a bomb that completely wrecked my head."

Mom snorted. "Yeah, he's got a habit of doing that. What did he say this time?"

"That Orion's been masking his scent since he was a literal kid."

Mom just gave a slow nod. "Okay... and?" She stared, waiting for the punchline. Like that didn't explain the whole damn thing.

"Mom, I loved him. But we weren't fated, so we walked away." I exhaled hard.

She nodded again, totally lost. "Okay? And?" She kept staring. I groaned. "Sweetie, I'm not following. Yes, he hides his scent." She rolled her wrists in the air. "But why does that change anything? You guys aren't mates."

I nodded fast. "He must've let it slip once, and I just forgot what it smelled like."

Mom's eyes slit. "Why does it sound like you're trying to convince yourself, not me?" She snatched my hand. "Spell it out for me, because I'm clearly missing a piece here. How did this even start?"

"We were talking about Cipher while Dad was out."

Mom nodded. "The internet guy?"

"Yeah."

"Why was your dad asking about him?"

"Because I have feelings for him." I slapped my hands over my face and groaned. "And after the whole Damon disaster, and then the mess with Orion..."

"Orion mess?" Mom frowned. "What mess with Orion?"

I groaned, dragging my hands down my face. "Back at school. Freshman year, I fell hard for him. He loved me too, but no spark, no mate bond. So we cut the cord to focus on our own shit." I waved my hands around. "I swore off guys. Period. But then Cipher somehow snuck past my guard. I don't even know how he did it."

Mom smirked. "Probably because he wasn't real."

My head whipped up. "Excuse me?"

Mom jabbed a finger at my phone. "He was just text on a screen. Hell, at first, you didn't even know he was a guy, right?" I nodded. "Exactly. You spent a whole year thinking Cipher was some girl, bonding, sharing secrets, only to find out he's a dude." She shook her head. "It's wild, but he was probably the only person who just listened without judging." I opened my mouth to defend my pack, but she waved me off. "I know, I know, your girls have your back. But it's different. Even when they support you, they're still judging. Cipher was just pixels. Even if he thought you were crazy, you'd never know unless he typed it. He was your escape hatch. You could vent, cry, and just exist. No Alpha duties, no leader pressure. You could be weak, because who cared?"

"Until it actually mattered," I muttered.

Mom nodded. "That's what a mate does. Zero judgment, total acceptance." She sighed. "But look at me, rambling. So, you guys were talking about Cipher, and then what?"

"And then the topic switched to Orion and our history." Mom leaned forward, hooked. I blew out a breath. "Damon hit me with that kudzu crap and triggered my heat. I literally begged Orion to touch me, but he shut me down."

Mom nodded. "Lycans are obsessed with fated mates. They save themselves for the bond." I nodded. "He didn't even try to take the edge off?"

I shook my head. "He loved me—he literally said it. I think he knew if he touched me, he'd lose control."

She nodded. "Makes sense. If he sparked a bond with you, the urge to seal it would have driven him insane." I must've looked totally blank, because she kept going. "Lycans aren't like regular wolves. We build physical bonds. You've felt it, right? Our beasts can literally reach into someone else's head."

My jaw dropped. "Wait, that's not normal?"

Mom shook her head. "It's the connections we forge. Pack, friends, lovers—they become actual physical bridges for mind-messages. Once the bridge is built, a wolf can cross it, sure. But only Lycans can build the damn thing in the first place. That's why regular wolves have to rely on mind-links."

"Are you for real?"

Mom nodded, letting out a chuckle. "So his beast was probably fighting like hell not to claim you, and he bailed to keep you safe. But again, why does this matter? You said you guys aren't mates."

I nodded. "You're missing the exact same blind spot I had until Dad pointed it out."

"What blind spot?"

"Because of Damon, I've been masking my scent. And Orion's been doing the exact same thing."

Mom blinked, stunned. "Hold up. You mean you two have never actually smelled each other?"
