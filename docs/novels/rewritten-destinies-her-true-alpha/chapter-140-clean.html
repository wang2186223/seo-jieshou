Chapter 140

Orion shook his head, walking to the back of the classroom. He hopped onto his desk. "Look, this isn't my usual class, but we need to clear some shit up." He rubbed his face. "We have a hierarchy for one simple reason. The strong shield the weak. The higher your rank, the more lives you're responsible for."

"Yeah, well, Alphas need to realize there are cleaner ways to get info," a guy named Todd chimed in.

I snorted. "Oh, really? Enlighten me. What's your move?"

"Excuse me?" Todd spun around, glaring at me.

"You said there are better ways. So, what's your play? The Alpha's life was on the line. We had zero clue how deep the betrayal went. A day later, we got hit by an organized rogue attack—rogues working together like a real pack. Without the intel I dragged out, my dad is dead, our pack is overrun, and everyone's morale is crushed. Oh, and they targeted women and kids. So, what's your move in my shoes?"

"I would've investigated," he muttered, tossing the words out.

"I did. That's exactly how I figured out who to interrogate. But sure, let's play your game. You investigate, and you get jack shit. All you know is some she-wolf is messing with the Alpha."

He turned fully to face me. "Messing with him how, exactly?"

"Making him fall head over heels for her."

"And why is that a bad thing?" He scoffed.

"Because the Alpha already has a fated mate." The room erupted into whispers.

"Wait. You're saying some she-wolf actually broke a goddess-blessed mate bond?"

"Damn right. We knew she was doing something, we just couldn't pin it down. It got so bad the Alpha attacked his own pup and fought his own wolf." I leaned forward, propping my chin on my hand. "So, your dad snaps. He almost kills you. He's acting completely erratic. The pack is in deep shit, you have one suspect, and zero answers. What do you do?"

"Talk to her family."

"They know absolutely nothing, or they've been ordered to shut up. Sure, if you're strong enough, you can break that command."

Orion shook his head. "Depending on the strength of the command and the wolf's rank, you shatter that block, you might just scramble their brain." I nodded.

I pointed at Orion. "Exactly. Too risky. What's your next move?"

"I don't know," he muttered, getting flustered.

"Come on, Todd. Your pack is in danger. Over a hundred people are counting on you to figure this out and save the pack—and your own father. A strong Alpha is the backbone of the pack. What's your next move?"

"I don't know!" He looked around for backup, but everyone avoided his gaze.

"Say you freeze. The next day, the attack hits. You lose all the kids, and a bunch of mates. How do you feel?"

"Bad."

"Everyone would blame you, Todd. You could've stopped it all. How do you tell their families? Their mates? How do you justify that?"

Todd shot a look at Orion, but the king's face was a total blank. "I don't know, okay!" he screamed.

I leaned back in my chair. "None of us do, Todd. That's the cold hard truth. But these are the choices Alphas face. We weigh our dignity, our morals, our very souls against the odds, and we make a call." I wiped my face. "I didn't like scaring that she-wolf. I didn't like hurting her. But doing that saved my dad. And the next day, he was fighting right beside me when we got hit. I almost died saving a family. I fought off three wolves to buy two kids enough time to run, knowing damn well I wasn't making it out."

"That's your job," he snorted.

"Damn right it is. It's my job to protect the weak. To back up the pack. To save kids, women, and guys like you who sit back and judge me because you don't have to make the hard calls. You get to sit here in class and call me a monster because I tortured a woman. But the truth is, I already weighed the cost. I became a monster so four little kids could wake up and have parents. I traded my soul to protect my dad, my pack, and those kids. And honestly, Todd? I'd do it again in a heartbeat." Orion gave a single nod.

"We've all made choices that destroy us to save others." Orion stood up and walked around his desk. "Now, let's talk python." He clicked the screen and started his lecture.
