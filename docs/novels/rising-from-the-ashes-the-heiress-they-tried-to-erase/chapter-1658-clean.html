And clearly, so had most of the room. That ten-billion opening price instantly eliminated nearly ninety percent of the bidders. Even representatives from international consortiums leaned toward one another, whispering rapid calculations about risk and projected returns. With the economy wobbling, injecting ten billion in cash into a stalled megaproject was a gamble bordering on reckless.

For several long seconds, no paddle moved.

From her seat in the front row, Rosanna turned to scan the hall, a frosty smile tugging at her lips. The land was prime—but the price was brutal. Unless someone had truly bottomless pockets, who would sink money into a stalled project at that price?

“Go ahead. Let’s see which fool takes the bait,” she muttered under her breath.

But just as the hall fell into a tense hush, a deep voice cut through the silence.

“Eleven billion!”

Heads whipped around. It was Dominic, seated at the far end of the second row, paddle raised, his expression perfectly composed. This land was crucial to the North New District’s public infrastructure plans. Allowing it to go unsold wasn’t an option—and letting an unscrupulous developer seize control was completely out of the question. Those were orders from the higher-ups.

“Oh? The gentleman bids eleven billion!” the auctioneer exclaimed, eyes gleaming.

Rosanna shot Dominic a sideways glance and let out a derisive laugh. “Well, old geezer, looks like you’ve poured your life savings into this one. Eleven billion for a dying project? Try not to bankrupt yourself.” Her tone dripped with mockery—though beneath it flickered something sharper. Envy. Apparently, the old man had deeper pockets than she had assumed.

“Twelve billion!” A prominent property magnate from Ricmont lifted his paddle without hesitation.

“Thirteen billion!” The Wall family representative followed swiftly.

The atmosphere ignited. Bids fired back and forth across the hall without pause, the figures climbing in dizzying leaps until, in what felt like no time at all, the price hit twenty-five billion—already brushing up against the land’s assessed market value.

The energy shifted. Caution crept in.

Dominic’s brows drew together. Paddle poised in his hand, he hesitated. Government funds didn’t materialize out of nowhere—twenty-five billion was already straining the allocation. These people had lost their minds.

The air in the auction hall grew heavy, almost suffocating. Chairs creaked. Several bidders were hunched over their phones, whispering urgent calculations. Sweat dotted foreheads. Paddles hovered uncertainly in midair.

Then the Wall family representative rose to his feet. He lifted three fingers, inhaled sharply—ready to call out thirty billion.

But before a sound could leave his mouth, Maia moved.

She had been seated quietly in the third row all this time. Now she raised her paddle, and in a voice calm enough to chill the room, she spoke first.

“Thirty billion.”

The auction hall seemed to freeze.

Silence crashed down like a hammer. The Wall family representative staggered, clutching his chest as he sank back into his seat. He had just scraped together the nerve to push to thirty billion—their ceiling, their absolute limit—and she had called it without a moment’s hesitation. He couldn’t outbid her.

Every eye in the venue snapped toward Maia. For a moment, several people genuinely wondered if they had misheard.

“Twenty-five to thirty in one jump? What is she doing?”
