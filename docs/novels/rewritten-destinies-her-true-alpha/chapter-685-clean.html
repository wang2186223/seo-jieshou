Chapter 650

All eyes snapped to Luna. Her pupils rolled back, leaving nothing but creepy, milky white. Morrigan dropped her chin. "Oracle," she muttered.

A smirk stretched across Luna’s kid face. "Hey, Mother," she said, her voice totally different. "Been a minute."

Morrigan managed a tight smile. "Phoebe treating you okay?"

Luna grinned wider. "Yeah. Snatching me from Helios saved me a hell of a lot of agony." She gave a stiff, tiny bow. "Never got to say thanks. Not properly, anyway." She straightened up and locked eyes with me. "Guess I can pay you back now."

"Pay me back how?" I stared at my kid, but honestly? I felt completely numb. My mate was dead, and my heart was dead right along with him. Then the guilt hit me like a truck. God, she deserved a real mother, not a hollow shell.

Luna cocked her head. "Drop the guilt. Seriously. You're way tougher than you think right now. Even if your guy stayed stuck with the goddess, you'd still step up for this kid." She gave me a firm nod. "The love's still in there, mama. You're just too numb to feel it." She whipped around to face the gods. "Listen up. We can still pull the King back."

Phoebe hit the dirt, hard. "Shaman, you for real?"

Luna nodded. "Look, you run the show when it comes to wolf souls and death, right?" She waited for Phoebe's quick nod. "So, pull his soul back." Phoebe nodded again, eyes wide.

My breath hitched. But the goddess looked straight at me. "Yeah, I can grab his soul. But I can't jumpstart his heart. Shadow's soul would just be a prisoner inside a rotting corpse."

"No way," I choked out, shaking my head violently. I looked at Luna. "That's a sick kind of hell. He doesn't deserve that."

Luna smiled softly. "I'd never ask you to do that to him. Or yourself." She looked over at Rhea. "Get Pluto on the line."

Rhea scowled. "Why the hell would we call him?"

"Simple. Phoebe owns the wolf side. But Pluto? He owns the human souls and the flesh." Luna pointed at me. "But you're the one who's gotta talk him into it."

My jaw hit the floor. "With what? I don't have anything he'd want."

Rhea let out a heavy sigh. "He's a cocky, moody bastard." She took a deep breath and yelled, "Pluto! Get your ass out here!" A shockwave blasted outward, and something deep inside my soul shuddered.

I gasped. A pitch-black shadow bubbled up from the dirt. Out stepped this pale guy with dark hair and a neat little beard. He was wearing a sharp three-piece suit—totally ridiculous next to all these ancient gods. He flicked his cuffs, scanning the trees. "Elowen," he said, looking at Rhea. "What's the emergency?"

Rhea gave a stiff nod. Her vibrant green skin turned a sickly yellow, like just standing near this death god was draining her life force. "Yeah, I called."

Pluto glanced toward where Orion's soul was. "Huh. Just saw this guy."

My heart leaped. "Is he okay?" I scrambled forward on my knees. "Is he safe? Tell me!" I reached out, grabbing his expensive suit pants. The second my fingers touched the fabric, a jolt shot through me. I gasped. The god raised an eyebrow.

Pluto yanked his leg back, breaking my grip, then squatted down in front of me. He hooked a finger under my chin, tilting my face up, studying me. "Well, well. Interesting." He stared for a second, then glanced over at Orion's lifeless body. "Orion's fine. He got a VIP pass to the Elysian Fields. Spent his whole life bleeding for his people. Good guy, good king."

A pathetic whimper escaped my throat. His eyes snapped back to me. "You're a wolf?" I nodded. "A Lycan?" I nodded again, desperate. He looked up at Morrigan. "She one of yours?" Morrigan nodded. "Very interesting," he muttered. He let go of my chin and stood up. "So, why am I here?" he asked Rhea, but she was just staring at me.

"It was me," I croaked. Pluto looked down, waiting. I stared at my trembling, dirty hands, trying to find the words. "Look, I don't have time to explain the whole mess. We're out of time. My link to Orion's soul is snapping. Once it goes, he's gone forever." I forced myself to look him in the eye. "You don't owe me squat. I get it. But I'm begging you. Please. Bring him back."

He snorted. I lunged, grabbing his leg again. Another electric shock zapped my arm, but I didn't let go. "I'll do anything. Whatever you want. As long as it's not evil and I can physically do it. Take my life instead. Just give me back my mate." I swallowed hard, fighting the lump in my throat. "I've lived two damn lives, and I only just found him." I yanked on his pants. "Please. Just give him back. You said it yourself—he's a good man, a good king. We're dead meat without him."

He dropped back into a squat, his finger hooking my chin again. Those pitch-black eyes bored into mine. "You can't trade your life for his, kid."

"Why the hell not?" I grabbed his wrist. Another shockwave rattled my bones.

"Because the math doesn't work," he said. "You can't trade five souls for two."

A collective gasp ripped through the crowd.

"You've got three souls inside you. Your kid has two. That's a bad deal."

My hand slapped over my belly. Behind me, my mother let out a choked sob. "She's pregnant?" she whispered, but I tuned her out.

"What do I have to do?" I begged.

He just stared. "The trade has to be equal. And it has to be willing."

"Then take me."
