Chapter 229

Something brushed my shoulder. I hit the dirt, hard.

"Come back... I'm freezing," a voice hissed.

Goosebumps exploded all over me. I scrambled, rolled, and bounced back to my feet. I spun around. There she was. Or whatever the hell she’d become.

Her hair was a nasty, matted mess of dreads and grease. Half of it was ripped out, showing patches of pasty white scalp. Her eyes? Totally hollowed out, just pure, starving desperation. That was her whole deal now—just hunger. Her lips were pulled back tight, baring her teeth in this creepy, permanent psycho-smile. She wore this fancy, jeweled black dress, but it just bagged off her bony, starved frame. Her soul was totally wiped out, but that damn dress looked brand new.

It hit me, and it was terrifying: she wanted my body. She wanted my life.

"Back off!" I screamed.

I bolted. She came right after me, and holy crap, she was fast. I scrambled over boulders and holes, but she was gaining. She was skittering like a giant spider, closing the gap. If she touched me, I was dead. I shuddered and threw myself sideways. Her fingernails grazed my shirt.

"Daisy! Juniper! Help!" I yelled, praying they'd hear me.

I tripped, backing up as she lunged. My back slammed into solid stone. I shrieked. She just cackled.

"So warm... you're so warm..."

She tripped over a rock, rushing too fast to grab me. I dodged left, but a sharp edge of rock—or maybe glass—sliced my leg open.

The second my blood hit the air, the freak went wild. Her tongue whipped out. Her black eyes rolled back. I screamed, scrambled up, and ran like hell. But my blood was like a power-up for her. Her shriveled skin started plumping up. She got faster, more alive.

"Tastes so good," she mumbled. Her slurred voice was suddenly getting clear.

She was literally sucking up every drop of my blood left behind. It made her stronger.

"Get the hell away!" I yelled.

"Oh, sweetie... I just want your warmth."

She sped up. Then, thank god, I saw it—daylight at the end of the tunnel. I pushed my wolf-speed to the absolute limit. But she was right on my ass.

She was gliding over the rocks like water now. This place was her playground. It clicked—she’d been rotting in these caves for ages. Who knows how long? But she knew every crack in this floor better than her own reflection.

I tripped again. I shrieked as her claws scraped my neck. I burst through the cave exit, thinking I was safe, but then—bam. A hard yank on my hair. I almost face-planted.

"No way. You're mine. So warm... My turn to be free. My turn to live."

She wrapped my hair around her fist, pulling tighter, and dragged me back into the dark. Panic set in. My scalp felt on fire, like it was bleeding, but she didn't care. My boots lost traction. I slid back.

"Let me go!" I thrashed.

"Nope." She smirked.

Her face had filled out so much her lips actually covered her teeth now. Honestly? She looked gorgeous. Those pitch-black eyes turned a warm golden-brown. Her hair was this glossy, wild mane of black curls.

"I did my time," she whispered. "Now it's your turn."

"I didn't break any rules!" I yanked back.

Suddenly, my hands shifted. My claws popped out. I almost wanted to cheer—it was the first easy shift I'd had in months.

"You're here without your shell. That's a rule-breaker. Shells come first."

I screamed, swung my claws, and sliced right through my own hair. Boom. Free. The sudden loss of tension sent her stumbling backward, still holding a fistful of my dark curls.

"No!" she shrieked.

I didn't waste a second. I didn't look back. I just ran. No plan, just pure survival, putting distance between me and that cave. I crested a hill and the landscape clicked—my dad's pack territory. I sprinted past the pack house, but then a brutal, bloodthirsty howl ripped through the air behind me. I froze.

Every muscle in my body wanted to lock up, but I knew I was dead meat if I stopped. So I kept hauling ass. But the deeper I got into the woods, the more lost I got. I had no clue where to go. I did the only thing left to do.

"Juniper! Daisy! Help me!" I screamed.

This time, a tug pulled at my gut, pointing right. I pivoted and ran like my life depended on it.
