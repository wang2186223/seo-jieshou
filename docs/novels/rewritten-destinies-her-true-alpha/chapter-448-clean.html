Chapter 448

I just stood there, staring. Mom didn't care. She shoved right past me, dumped the tray on the mattress, and splashed wine into two glasses. She whipped around, shoved a glass into my hand, and hopped right into my bed. "Up you get, kiddo. Move it."

Just like that, my tough-girl act shattered. Having her there did it. I kicked the door shut, walked around, and crawled in next to her. I took a massive gulp of wine, then slammed the glass onto the nightstand.

"You don't have to do this, you know," I muttered, looking her way.

She just smirked. "Duh." She yanked the covers over her knees and dragged the tray onto her lap. "I know, babe." She popped a cracker into her mouth, chewed slow, took a big swig of wine, and locked eyes with me. "But there's no way in hell I'm sitting in the living room while you're in here bawling your eyes out."

Her eyes drifted to the messy pile of letters between us. "Is this the culprit?" She snatched an open page and started reading. I watched her eyes dart back and forth. Then, she let the paper drop to her lap and stared at me. "Oh, honey..."

I grabbed my glass and chugged the rest of the wine. "It's just..." I shook my head, hard. "Reading this stuff... it hurts way worse than I thought it would." I held my empty glass out. She poured me another.

"Why are you even torturing yourself with these?" She grabbed another envelope. "Wait, are these not in order?" She frowned, totally lost.

I nodded. "He wrote every single week. But I never got them."

Mom stared down at the pile. "Why the hell not?"

"Why did he write?" I shrugged. "Probably hated how we ended things."

"No, dummy." She smacked my arm. "Why didn't you *get* them?"

I wiped a tear off my cheek. "Because Dad decided I was better off without them."

Mom gasped, sucking in air. "Ugh. Your father and his god-complex." She sighed, looking at the unopened stack. "What do you need me to do?"

I stared at the next envelope. "Just stay." I picked it up. "I don't think I have the stomach for this." My hands were shaking like crazy as I tried to tear the seal.

Mom snatched it right out of my grip. "Then don't. I've got this." She slid the letter out and flattened it.

"Dear Lyra." Her voice went soft. I slumped back against the headboard. "It's been a month since I last saw you, and I know for a fact I can't survive without you." Mom took a quick sip of wine, her lips thinning. "Your scent is everywhere. It's like a ghost. Even when it makes no sense, it's there. I turn a corner, and boom, it's on the wind. Is it actual magic, or just you? Someone is hunting me down, using you as bait. And damn, it's working."

Mom paused and looked up.

"I bet you're wondering why I'm ghosting you. If I'm this crazy about you, why hide? Simple. Your dad."

I let out a low growl. Mom's lips twitched into a smirk.

"He's got a plan. I fucking hate it, but it actually makes sense. And I hate myself for agreeing." Mom sighed, shaking her head. "Classic. Your father and his damn master plans."

She gave me a sad look and kept reading.

"Your dad thinks this is the perfect cover to play along with those council girls. Raven, Astrid, and that whole crowd. He knows someone is messing with my head using magic. He can feel it, but doesn't know who's behind it. Honestly, it's the perfect trap. We can finally smoke out the enemy. But please, if you do one thing, forgive me. I'm begging you. This silence is killing me. Orion."

Mom took a deep breath, folded the paper, and slid it back into the envelope.

I shook my head, rubbing my aching chest. "Why does this hurt so damn much?"

Mom sighed, reaching for the next letter. "Because he mattered to you."

"Huh?" I stared at her, then reached over and grabbed a cracker.

"He mattered. Big time. What he thought, his time, his feelings—you cared about all of it." She grabbed my hand and gave it a hard squeeze. "It hurts because you loved him. And honestly? You probably still do."

I growled. "I shouldn't. He wrecked me."

Mom sighed. "Well, from the looks of this, he didn't really have a choice."
