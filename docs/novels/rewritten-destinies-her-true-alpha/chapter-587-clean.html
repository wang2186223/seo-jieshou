Chapter 587

"Forget my story. That's for another night," Luna said. "I'm here to lay down some facts. Fifty lives. I've lived fifty damn lives. Some were a flash, some dragged on, with ages waiting in the dark while the Moon Celestia recycled souls for round two. But get this through your head: I never forgot my first mate. Sure, I ran into him in another life, but he was always the one."

Elara gasped, fighting for air as the last contraction ripped through her. "But... you had other guys, right?"

Luna gave a slow nod. "Yeah. Celestia hooked me up with a few others along the way."

"Doesn't that... hurt them?" Elara whimpered.

Luna just smiled, shaking her head. "Kid, love isn't some pie you run out of." She crawled onto the mattress, sliding right up to them. I watched this tiny girl mothering two wolves who'd been around way longer than her current body. "You're gonna love a lot of souls, in a lot of ways. One love doesn't kill another. You'll always love Rhys Thorne, and he'll always love you. Even if you get a new mate down the road, you won't forget a damn thing about Rhys. He gave you these babies. A second shot at life doesn't wipe the slate clean. Celestia isn't that cruel. She wouldn't do that to you, or to Rhys, and she sure as hell wouldn't dump some random wolf on you who doesn't get who you are." She leaned in, pressing a quick kiss to Elara's forehead. "One last thing. Your mate is gonna watch over you. And if you get a new guy, he's gonna watch you fall for him, too." She snapped her gaze to Rhys. "Is that gonna make you stop loving her? Or your kids?"

Termen didn't even hesitate. He shook his head, hard. "Not a damn chance. Nothing stops that."

"See?" Luna turned back to Elara. "Flip the script. Say you die right now, but Rhys makes it. You want him and those babies rotting in loneliness forever?"

Elara shook her head, tears in her eyes. "No. I'd want them loved. I'd want my babies to know what a mother's love feels like."

Luna sat back on her heels. "Bingo." She slid off the mattress. "Love doesn't die just because someone checks out. It changes. It grows. Celestia won't hand you a new mate unless your soul is ready for it." She headed for the door. "You're her kids. She wants you happy." She paused, locking eyes with me. "Even when it feels like she's screwing you over." The door clicked shut. I stared at the wood, her words rattling around in my brain. A cold, nasty dread crawled up my spine, settling right at the base of my skull like a killer migraine.

Suddenly, Elara shrieked, her body jerking violently. The sharp, metallic stench of fresh blood hit my nose. Instantly, my mom dove back under the sheets. "Alright, ready or not, this kid is coming!" She whipped her hand out—it was slick with bright red blood. Daisy scrambled over, shoving a towel into her hands. Mom glared up at Elara. "Look at me. I know you're scared, but we are out of time. You have to push, sweetie. Now!" She ducked back under the blanket. "Push!"

Elara locked her hands around her knees. Rhys and I threw our weight against her back, grabbing her legs to hold them steady so she could put everything into it. She let out a raw, throat-tearing scream, sweat drenching her face as she strained.

"Stop! Stop pushing!" my mom shrieked. The room went dead silent.

"What? What is it?" Rhys's voice cracked, pure panic bleeding through.

"The cord's wrapped around the neck. Don't move. Give me a second!"

Nobody breathed. We just watched her hands work under the sheet.

Elara's eyes locked onto mine, wide with terror. The scent of pure fear flooded the room. "Another one," she gasped. "It's coming."

"Hold it," I urged, shoving my hand into hers. "Squeeze me. Break my hand if you have to, but do not push until my mom says so." Elara grabbed my hand like a lifeline, her grip instantly crushing. Her face twisted in agony, another scream ripping from her throat. She was vibrating, fighting her own body's urge to push—once that train starts, you can't just stop it. She thrashed her head side to side, panting, whimpering a desperate prayer. "Please, God, no. Please."

"Almost got it," Mom grunted. The contraction faded, and Elara's grip loosened. I felt the shattered bones in my hand snapping back into place, clicking together while Elara sobbed.

"Mom, hurry up! She can't hold back another one!" Panic was clawing at my chest, but I forced it down. Focus. Just focus. Elara's fingers clamped down on mine again. Oh, hell. Here we go.

"Almost..." Mom muttered.

"Mom!" I yelled. The wave hit. Elara screamed, a sound of pure agony.

"Got it! It's clear! Push!" Mom yelled.

Elara let out one final, roaring bellow, then completely collapsed against me, totally spent.

"Elara?" Rhys leaned over her, his face pale. "Hey, baby? Look at me."

Total silence. Just the wet drip of blood on the floor and Elara's ragged breathing. Then, the most beautiful sound in the world shattered the quiet.

A sharp, loud baby cry.

Mom's head popped out from under the sheet, a streak of blood smeared across her cheek. She grinned. "It's a boy."
