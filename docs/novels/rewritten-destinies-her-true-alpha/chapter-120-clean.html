Chapter 120

The guy just froze. I locked eyes with him. He was burning up inside—I could see it—but I didn't blink. Just kept staring.

"Yeah, right. Real fair," I said, spinning around to look Blair dead in the eye.

"Oh, so now we're talking about fair?" Blair snapped, glaring daggers. "Like how you treated my mom like some low-life omega? Or how she's finally done taking your crap? Huh?" I just cocked my head. "Look, neither of you has given me one good reason to step in and tell her to back off. So, hell no. I'm not helping either of you." I grabbed my phone and my mug. Took a slow sip of coffee, then went right back to scrolling. Blair was practically vibrating with rage right next to me.

"Then get the fuck out!" Blair shoved her chair back so hard it crashed onto the floor. She stood there, chest heaving, panting like a crazy person. "Take your whore of a mother and get the hell out of our house." She glared, but I just set my coffee down. Slow. Then I looked over at Ronnie.

I let out a slow smirk. Ronnie's face went totally white. "Now, why on earth would I pack up and leave a house that I actually own?"

"Bullshit! This is my house!" Blair shrieked. "The Alpha gave this place to my dad!" She stomped over, lunging to grab my arm. I snatched her wrist mid-air and squeezed. Hard.

Blair froze, her hand trapped in my grip. I looked past her to Ronnie. "Hey, Ronnie. Your kid's got things mixed up again." I cranked up the pressure. She whimpered.

"Please," he muttered under his breath. I couldn't tell what he was begging for.

"Please what, exactly?" I tilted my head back, looking straight up at Blair. I wanted to see her face drop when she heard this. "Please let Blair go? Or please don't tell her that the Alpha was about to seize this place a few years back? You know, because the pack was bleeding cash and he wanted to rent it out? He wanted us all packed up and back in the pack house. So, I stepped up. Bought it with the cash my dad sends me. Half a million bucks, and boom—the roof over your head became mine. Sure, it's technically under my mom's name since I was just a kid, but your dad and Magnus know damn well how it went down."

Blair whipped her head toward her dad. He looked away, guilty as hell. That was all the proof she needed. "No way. You're lying."

"Oh, I'm dead serious." I stood up, forcing her to take a step back with every inch I rose. "And let's get one more thing straight. Your dad let you act like you're some big shot, way above your actual pay grade. Even the Alpha knows what's up, and honestly? He thinks you're pathetic."

"Lyra, come on..." Ronnie pleaded, shaking his head.

But I was done. "Save it, Ronnie. I've been way too nice up until now, but everyone has a limit." I stared him down until he flinched and looked away again. "Get this through your head, Blair. This is my place. My mom tolerated your crap only because of a deal she made with your dad. But since he's backing out on his word, the deal is officially off."

"What the hell does that mean?" Ronnie snapped, looking up. But he didn't see me. He saw Umbra staring back.

"You're a joke of a wolf," I growled. "Couldn't even hold onto your beta rank. You have zero clue where you actually stand. So let me school you on your place."

"Lyra, don't, please."

I slammed my aura down like a ton of bricks. Blair hit the floor instantly, face smashed against the hardwood. "Blair Farrow, you don't move from that spot until I walk out of this house. Every time I walk into a room, you drop to your knees. And don't you dare speak a word to me until I say you can."

"Lyra!" Ronnie scrambled up and rushed to his daughter. "Call it off. Now!" I looked down at him, Umbra rumbling deep in my chest. "Who the hell do you think you are, ordering me around?" I snarled.

Ronnie stammered, looking up at me in shock. "She's your sister. You can't do this to her."

I let out a dry laugh and sat back down. "Sister? Since when did she ever treat us like family? She treated my mom like a maid and me like some annoying pest. Well, that shit ends today." I turned back to my mug. Ronnie stood up and started yelling for my mom.

She walked right in, holding two plates loaded with waffles and bacon. She set one down in front of me. Ronnie pulled out his chair, thinking the other plate was his, but she just sat in her own seat and dug right in.

"Elara, you need to—"

"I don't have to do a damn thing, Ronnie," she snapped. "You and your kid are on your own now. Go eat at the pack house." She dug back into her food, and we both ignored them and kept eating.
