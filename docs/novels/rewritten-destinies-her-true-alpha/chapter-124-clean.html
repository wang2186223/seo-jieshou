Chapter 124

Mom gave me a nod, locking eyes with me. "Look, you’re not dumb with cash. I just want to make sure your pockets are lined enough to do whatever you want." She chewed on her bottom lip, stressing over it.

I spun back to Eleanor. "Hey, what’s the normal damage people put on these cards?"

"Oh, let's see." Eleanor tapped away on her keyboard, then nodded. "Betas and the lower ranks usually drop twenty-five to forty grand. But the Alphas? Yeah, they go way higher."

"Why's that?" I asked. Beside me, Mom was literally counting on her fingers. I couldn't help but snort.

"Well, they get access to some exclusive campus clubs. Plus..." Eleanor shut her mouth, trailing off.

"Plus what? Spill," I nudged.

She leaned in close, dropping her voice to a whisper. "Look, it depends on the Alpha. Some of them just blow through their cash at the clubs like crazy."

"You keep mentioning these clubs. What are they, anyway?"

"A couple of sports bars and a dance spot. Only ranked wolves can even get through the door. But the Alphas have this VIP section, and it’s stupid expensive. The show-offs just dump their cash there. The nicer ones, though? They actually sponsor kids."

"Sponsor kids? What do you mean?"

Eleanor let out a heavy sigh. "Some of the unranked wolves are flat broke. Like, zero dollars. They have to grind at campus jobs just to buy a damn meal."

Mom clapped a hand over her mouth. My stomach dropped. "Wait. They’re here to get an education, and they have to stress about starving?" I whipped my head toward Mom. She just shook her head, looking sick. "That is freaking awful."

"Look, I know you can't give me names," I said, pulling my phone out and punching in my dad's number. "But can you check how many kids currently have a big fat zero on their cards?"

"Sure thing." Eleanor started tapping the keys while my dad's line rang.

"Hey, baby?" Dad’s deep rumble came through the speaker. Beside me, Mom literally shivered. "Everything okay?"

"Not really, Dad," I said, taking a deep breath. "I need you to wire me a ton of money. Like, right now."

"Wait, what?" Dad chuckled. "Why?"

"I'm at the admin office. We're about to load up my meal card."

Dad straight-up busted out laughing. "They’re still pulling that scam?" He cackled for a solid minute, clearly cracking himself up. "Alright, alright. What’s the damage for your food?"

"It’s not for my food, Dad. I’m worried." The line went dead silent.

"There are three hundred low-ranked and unranked wolves with absolutely nothing on their food accounts," Eleanor whispered. I gave her a quick nod.

I covered the mic and whispered back, "How much for a basic three-meals-a-day plan plus textbooks for one semester?"

She clicked around, her face scrunching up. "Roughly ten grand."

"Dad... I need three million dollars."

On the other end, Dad literally choked on his drink. Coughing fit. "Okay, hold on. Explain." I heard him mumble something to Finnian, who also started hacking and coughing. Dad laughed again, but it was tense.

"I just found out that the lower and unranked wolves have to work slave hours on campus just to eat, instead of actually studying."

"What?" Dad’s voice cracked. "Seriously?"

"Yeah. Seriously."

"When the hell did that start? Tuition used to cover a basic meal plan." Eleanor’s eyes went wide, and she looked away fast.

"They axed the built-in meal plan about ten years ago," Eleanor chimed in. I did the quick math in my head. I flipped the phone onto speaker and shoved it closer to her to make things easier.

"Alpha Magnus took over as Dean ten years ago," Dad growled. His voice went pitch-black. He was pissed.

"Yes, sir. That's correct," Eleanor muttered, refusing to look me in the eye.

"How much is just the bare-minimum food plan?" Dad's voice was deadly calm now. I knew that tone.

Eleanor swallowed hard. "About six thousand."

I pulled the phone back. "Dad, I asked for the food plan *and* textbooks for the semester."

"Right, books and the basics. So that's..."

"Ten grand a head," I cut in.

"And how many kids in the whole school are short for the semester?" Dad asked. God, he was always three steps ahead of me.

Eleanor, bless her, was practically shaking in her boots. "Give me a sec." She hammered the keyboard for a minute. "Okay. Three hundred have zero balance. And another two hundred are sitting way below the basic living cost."

"Mrs...?" Dad started.

"Sandlewood," she supplied.

"Eleanor. You were the secretary back when I was a student, right?"

"Yes, Alpha. I was." She blushed bright red. Mom rolled her eyes with a smirk.

"Alright, Eleanor. What’s the grand total to bump every single one of those kids above the line?"

She clicked a few more times. We all heard her swallow hard. "Four million, seven hundred thousand, and thirty bucks."
