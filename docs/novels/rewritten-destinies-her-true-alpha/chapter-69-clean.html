Chapter 69

The guards dragged those three out. The medical crew did a quick once-over on Finnian and me, then cleared out. Finally. I flopped onto the mattress, totally spent. Finnian and Dad marched over, hovering right in front of me.

"You good?" Dad locked eyes with me.

I gave a weak nod. "Just dead tired," I muttered, waving a hand at the two of them. "How are you guys holding up?"

Dad turned, slapping a heavy hand on Finnian’s shoulder. "Look, I trust you with my life. But I gotta ask. You 100% sure?" Dad’s voice cracked with a tiny sliver of hope. Unbelievable. Even after this shitshow, he was still hoping for a mistake.

"Am I sure about the guy who literally put a blade in my gut?" Finnian let out a dry, exhausted laugh. "Yeah, Gary. I’m damn sure." He slumped down on the edge of the mattress right next to me. "I went to check on him right after the Agora fight. He was lying in bed, completely healed up. I told him about the raid, and he played dumb. Acted like he didn't know a thing. But once the clinic started filling up with bleeding pack members, he threw his clothes on." He glanced my way. "Then your dad mind-linked me. Said you were in here, totally knocked out. I lost my absolute shit. I went on a manhunt."

"Hunting?" I nudged his shoulder with mine. "For who?"

"Morgana," he spat. "No way in hell was I letting that bitch walk after she unleashed those rogues on us. Especially not after what she did to you." His fists balled up, knuckles white. "Cyrus offered to back me up. I said fine. We swept the town, caught her scent, and tracked it straight into the woods." Finnian locked eyes with Dad. "The second we were deep enough in the trees—where nobody could hear a damn thing—he jumped me. We were both in wolf form. I was kicking his ass, but then he shifted back to human. So, like an idiot, I shifted too."

"Ron! Are you crazy?"

"I know, I know! Stupid move," he snapped, jumping up to pace the room. The guy was practically vibrating with rage. "But I needed answers! He was our brother. We grew up together. Out of nowhere, he’s trying to kill me? I had to know why. But the bastard set a trap. The second I shifted back, he drove a blade into me. And he laughed. Laughed! Said he was sorry, but he knew I'd never betray you." Finnian grabbed Dad by the shirt. "This whole thing? It was a hit on you, Gary. Not the pack. He literally told the rogues to keep the body count low. The entire raid was just a cover to wipe out you and Lyra. Once he spilled that, I shifted and bolted."

"And you ran straight back here?" Dad shook his head, totally stunned.

"Duh. I had to warn you," Finnian said, giving me a look that basically said *your dad is a total moron*. Honestly, he wasn't wrong.

"Dad... hate to break it to you, but you're the Alpha." I let out a dry, ridiculous laugh.

"I'm aware of that, kiddo."

"And you're Uncle Finnian's best friend. Plus, his Alpha. It's his job to protect the pack, and you're his brother. Of course he came straight to you. If the tables were turned and he was the Alpha, wouldn't you do the exact same thing?"

Dad nodded slowly. "Yeah. Obviously."

Finnian and I threw our hands up in the air.

"Then why the hell are you so shocked he did it for you? We love you, Dad. You're the VIP of this pack. Especially to us." I threw my arms around his neck.

"Hey, my mate is my number one, you're number two," Finnian chimed in, slapping Dad on the back. "But for this pack? For our entire way of life? You're the whole damn show."

I pulled back, my face hardening. "We need to figure out why they backstabbed us."

"And we need to hunt down Morgana. Now."

But Dad just shook his head. "Morgana will crawl out of her hole when she's ready. And I don't give a damn about their 'reasons'. They betrayed us. That's all I need to know. We'll just double the patrols and watch our backs."

"Dad, you can't just sweep this under the rug!" But he was already backing away. He exchanged a heavy, silent look with Finnian. "Dad! We need answers!"

"No, kiddo. We don't," Dad said flatly. "Finnian says the King is on his way. I'll handle this with him. This whole mess... it's a carbon copy of what happened back then."

"Back then?" I looked back and forth between them. "You mean... when the old king got murdered?"

Dad gave a slow, grim nod.

"Looks like we missed a few rats back then. And now they're starting up again." Dad walked over to the window, staring out into the dark. "But none of that matters right now."

"What do you mean it doesn't matter?"

"What matters is finishing that copy of your grandmother's book. And after that..."

"After that, what?" I hurried to the window. For a split second, his face crumbled in pure agony before he locked it down, turning his expression into a blank wall.

"And then, I'm packing you up and sending you home. You've learned everything you need to here. It's time to go back and face your past life."
