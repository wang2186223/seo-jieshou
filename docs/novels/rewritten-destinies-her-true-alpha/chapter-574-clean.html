Chapter 574

"Jesus, lady!" Gideon spun around, chest heaving. "Put a damn bell on!" He slapped a hand over his heart, trying to catch his breath. Juniper let out a fake cough to hide her laugh.

Grandma flashed a smug grin, then locked eyes with me. "Move it," she said, pointing right at the fissure. "Go." She faced the stone wall again. The second she stepped into the rock, that tiny split ripped open into a massive arch. "Clock's ticking!" her voice echoed. I didn't waste a second—I bolted down the stairs.

"Lyra!" Orion yelled. Right behind him, my mate and the rest of the crew scrambled down after me.

"Double time!" I shouted over my shoulder. That gut-wrenching panic flared up again, but it felt faint. I just knew—if that feeling died out completely, it meant my dad was dead.

"Where the hell does this lead?" Gideon barked. Any other day, I would've cracked up. I glanced back, caught my own foot, and ate dirt with a hard grunt.

"Lyra!" Orion was there in a flash. His arms locked around me, hauling me back up. "You good?"

"*Iccas lumin torcanta*." I tossed a glowing orb into the air. It hovered overhead, lighting up the stone wall. I reached out, tracing the massive split in the rock.

"Hey, Lyra?" Juniper's quiet voice snapped me out of it. "What's caught your eye?"

I ran my fingers along the jagged edge. Luna's warning echoed in my head: *The crack is open.* "This wasn't here before," I whispered.

"Uh, is this whole place gonna collapse?" Gideon scanned the ceiling, gulping so loud it echoed. "Because getting crushed to death sounds like a shitty way to die."

I spun around at his words, a weird spark firing in my brain. "Wait, what?" I pulled away from the fissure, but a sharp rock sliced my finger. "Ah, shit!" I hissed. I held my hand up to the light. Blood was gushing out—way too much for a tiny scratch.

A heavy, rumbling snap echoed. My vision blurred, and then—pitch black.

"Anyone there?" I croaked. Breathing hurt like hell. Every gasp felt like inhaling straight fire. The air was pure soup; even my wolf vision couldn't cut through the gloom. "*Iccas lumin torcanta*," I wheezed. A pathetic little spark sputtered to life.

Nothing but shattered boulders and swirling dust clouds. Off to my left, some faint groans and whimpers echoed, but the jagged rocks warped the sound. I couldn't tell how far away they were.

I groped around. Orion had been right beside me before the lights went out, but now my hands hit nothing but cold stone. I tried to shift my weight, and the brutal truth hit me—I was pinned. My brain finally registered the trap, and a scream ripped from my throat. Big mistake. The air vanished instantly. Dust coated my tongue, cutting my scream short and sending me into a violent coughing fit.

I tried to muster enough juice to shove the boulder off my left arm. No shot. I was completely drained, like I'd been stuck here for hours.

Hours? No way. I shook my head. We were just in the tunnel a minute ago... unless I got knocked out cold. I tried to clear my head, but the world just spun. "HELLO!" I shrieked. Pure panic clawed at my throat.

"Hang tight, Leo!" Finnian's voice cut through. "Don't freak out! You're burning through the oxygen! We're digging you out, just hold on!"

I slumped back against the rocks crushing me. My head banged against a sharp edge, making me wince. This was it. The end of the line. I wasn't making it out. "I'm sorry, Fiona... Lyra... guess we ran out of luck." My little spark of light flickered once, twice, and then my magic gave out. Total darkness swallowed me.

"Lyra!" My eyes snapped open. Orion was cradling my face. The truth hit me like a freight train, making my stomach do a violent flip. "Baby?" He pressed a hard kiss to my lips, then pulled back, face pale as a ghost. "You scared the absolute shit out of me. You just collapsed, and your eyes..."

"What happened?" My throat burned, raw and dry, like I'd been eating dirt.

"They went totally white. Just like Luna's," Juniper said, peeking over his shoulder. "Did you just have a vision?"

I blinked, trying to catch my breath. A hacking cough seized me. I shoved Orion away just in time to heave. A thick, black puddle of sludge splattered onto the ground between us.

"Why the hell is she puking up dirt?" Gideon squinted, leaning in.

"What do you mean?" Juniper snapped, looking up from beside me.

Gideon whipped out his phone and clicked on the flashlight. "Look at it. That's not food. That black crap in her vomit? It's straight-up soil." He glared at me while I gasped for air. "Why are you puking dirt, Lyra?"

"My dad..." I finally hacked up the last of it and scrambled to my feet. I stumbled blindly down the pitch-black tunnel. "My dad's buried alive."
