Chapter 1147

"Dad, please! You can't just dump me here!" Willow sobbed, her eyes raw and red. She was practically on her knees.

But Corbin shot her a look—pure, icy venom. She choked back her next sob and shut her mouth.

*Pfft.*

Someone snorted. The sound sliced right through the suffocating silence.

Heads snapped around. Everyone stared.

Lyra, who’d been chilling in the corner the whole time, pushed herself up. She shoved a hand into her pocket, totally relaxed. "Whoa there, Mr. Doyle," she said, her voice smooth. "Don't go cutting ties just yet."

Her red lips curled into a slow, lazy smirk. She pointed a finger at the giant screen behind her. "Seriously, the good stuff is just starting."

Corbin’s stomach dropped. A cold sweat broke out on his neck.

Lyra flashed her phone at him. Her pale fingers tapped the glass. *Tap, tap.*

Boom. The screen flickered. The security footage fractured into a million digital pieces, then reformed. Spreadsheets, bank statements, and tax documents flooded the display.

It was the dirty money. Years of Doyle Pharma’s black market cash, routed through dozens of shell companies and crypto nodes. Every single transaction was laid bare.

"Holy crap," someone whispered. "Is that their actual ledger?"

"Wait, look at those offshore accounts!"

"Who owns those? That’s literally millions of bucks!"

Corbin drained of color. His face went paper-white. No way. Their classified books... how the hell did she get this? Every single detail was there. His brain short-circuited. Only one word flashed in his mind: *Run.* Get out. Now. If he stayed, he was dead. His entire life was about to blow up.

He bolted. One step, two—

*Thwack!*

A plastic water bottle rocketed through the air, nailing him right in the back of the knee. His leg buckled. Corbin went down hard, eating dirt. He rolled over, groaning, and looked up.

"Running so soon?" Lyra strolled over. She stood over him like a queen, looking down with eyes like ice. "Come on, Mr. Doyle. We haven't even talked about those poor scholarship kids you used as lab rats."

Corbin scrambled to get to his feet. But the screen behind him flashed again.

What popped up next was pure nightmare fuel.

An underground bio-lab. Rows of iron cages. Endless hospital gurneys. People—skeletal, skin-and-bones—lay hooked up to IVs and tubes. Scientists in white coats paced around them, taking notes.

Documents scrolled by. Trial logs. Subject ID numbers. Side effects. Death certificates. Then, the faces. One by one, the students the Doyles had "sponsored" popped up. The paper trail was airtight. It proved exactly how they used charity as a cover to turn poor kids into human guinea pigs.

Mutation trial footage, lists of dead kids, photos of bodies mangled beyond recognition—it was all up there, huge and undeniable.

The press room exploded. Camera shutters went wild, sounding like machine guns. People started screaming curses.

"You sick bastards!"

"Are you freaking kidding me?!"

"Human testing? They actually did human testing?!"

Flat on his back, Corbin stared at the screen, shaking like a leaf. No. No way. This was buried so deep. How did she dig this up? He glared at Lyra, his eyes bloodshot, looking like he wanted to rip her throat out.

But Lyra didn't even blink. She just stood there, cool and bored, looking down at him like he was a piece of trash stuck to her shoe.
