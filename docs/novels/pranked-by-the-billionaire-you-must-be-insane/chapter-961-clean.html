She’d dragged them out of the pitch-black. Now, they had to keep the fire burning themselves. Light their own way. Pull the rest of the crew out of the dark.

"Hell yeah, Captain!" they barked back, eyes locked and loaded.

Dinner at the Lancaster place that night. Cozy lights, the smell of a massive home-cooked feast. Lyra was mid-bite.

Her screen lit up. No caller ID. She squinted at it, already knowing the asshole on the other end. She dropped her fork, swiped accept, and held the phone to her ear. Didn't say a word.

A slimy laugh buzzed through the speaker. "Hey there, Miss Lancaster. Long time no see. Miss me?" God, his voice made her skin crawl.

Lyra kept her face deadpan. "Sebastian."

"Aw, you remembered. I'm touched," he purred, sounding way too turned-on. "Hear your little loser squad is all geared up. You really that desperate to take the Gauntlet?"

She wasn't playing. "Cut the crap. What do you want?"

"Feisty. I like that. Makes me want to break you even more," he whispered, his voice thick with creepy craving. "So, when do I get you behind closed doors? Just you, me, and whatever I feel like doing to you?"

Lyra rolled her eyes, thumb hovering over the red button. He must've sensed it because his laugh went sharp. "Whoa, hold on. Don't hang up."

"We both have a lot riding on this gig. You trashed my family's auction, remember? No way in hell I'm letting you cruise through the Gauntlet. You're not ruining my life twice."

He stopped, his voice dropping into the gutter. "But hey... you want in? Come over tonight. Keep me warm. Beg a little. Make me happy, and maybe—just maybe—I'll let your sad little team actually step on that stage. You know you've always had me hooked, babe."

Lyra's eyes went dead cold. "Go screw yourself," she spat, and killed the call.

She slammed the phone face down, radiating pure ice. The Doyles were begging to get wrecked.

"Hey, you okay? Who was that?" Edward asked. He'd watched her face go totally dark the second she picked up. Around the table, everyone stopped eating, staring at her, worried sick.

Ethan pushed up his gold-rimmed glasses. "Look, Lyra. You can handle your own, we know that. But say the word, and I'll make them disappear."

They weren't stupid. If something made Lyra look that pissed, it wasn't some minor drama. It was serious trouble. But they had her back, period.

"Don't worry about it," Lyra said, her face softening as she looked around. She forced a tiny smile. "Just some idiot trying to scare me off the Gauntlet tomorrow."

Ethan’s eyes went cold behind his glasses. "The Doyles?"
