Chapter 42

Obsidian glared up, voice like ice. "Pup? What the hell happened to your face? Who did that to you?"

"You did." I felt my cheek swell, warm blood dripping from my eye and nose.

"What?" He shook his head, dazed. "What's happening?"

"Yeah, I'd like to know what the hell is going on, too. Why was your old man trying to kill you over Morgana?" Finnian dropped into a chair. I let go of my dad and let him stand.

"I went to see my dad. Can't even remember why now. But I heard voices." I wiped the blood off my cheek, rubbing the skin as it started to heal. "He asked what she was doing. She said 'bonding.' My gut screamed. I kicked the door off its hinges. She was pouring some junk down his throat. So I tackled her."

"And then?" Finnian pointed at my dad. I nodded.

"He went after you?" Obsidian asked.

"Yeah." I nodded again.

"What the actual hell is going on?"

"Obsidian, I need the Book of Shadows."

"Right. Give me a sec." Obsidian stood up and bolted out. I looked at Finnian. He grimaced.

"Man, you're healing, but that looks like pure hell. That nasty bruise on your neck? That's gonna take the longest."

"Probably." I massaged my throat, coughing to clear it. Obsidian walked back in, shoving the book into my hands. His face was totally off. "Hey. What's wrong?"

"Last night, when I was working with the book, I got this weird gut feeling. I changed the safe combo. Blocked your dad from seeing it. Just in case."

I nodded. "Smart move."

"Well, the safe is covered in claw marks. Someone tried to rip it open. Maybe it was your dad and he blocked my memory, or maybe someone snuck in while we were in here." He shook his head. "We can't keep this book here. Not until we fix this mess."

I grabbed the book, nodding. "I'll keep it."

"Good." Obsidian sank into a chair, leaning back. "I'm a danger to the pack right now. I need the room."

"The room?" I looked at Finnian. The guy had gone totally white.

"When your dad senses your mom... with your stepdad... he locks himself in there." Finnian turned his head slowly to face me.

"It's a soundproof cage deep in the woods," Obsidian said, staring straight at me. "Only Finnian knows the spot. He keeps an eye on me."

"Why?" I asked.

"Because I put my hands on you. I can feel him clawing for control right now. Whatever crap Morgana did to us, I won't let it put you in danger." Obsidian growled, teeth bared. "Keep both of us locked up until Lyra finds a fix."

"You're crazy, Obsidian. You can't do this."

"I can and I will. Your dad would say the exact same thing if he was in his right mind." He shot a look at Finnian. "Let's go."

"Alright."

"Uncle Finnian, stop him. You can't let him do this!" I pleaded.

"He's right, Lyra. If he hurts you, he'll never live it down. I literally just walked in on him trying to choke the life out of you. For your own good, and the pack's, he's going under lock and key until you find a fix. I'm not risking you. Or anyone else." Finnian stood up. Obsidian followed him out the door.

What the actual hell? I snatched the book, bolted upstairs to my room, and slammed the lock home. I had to find out what she did to him. Because that guy? That wasn't my dad. I started shaking, the memory of his fingers squeezing my throat rushing back.

*That wasn't him.* Umbra's voice echoed, taking the edge off my panic. But my hands still trembled as I cracked open the Book of Shadows.

I scanned every single page. Twice. Nothing. Nothing explained why he'd snap like that. "No way. That wasn't him. Something is seriously messed up." I shook my head, trying to clear it, tracing back through everything I'd seen since I arrived.

I flipped back, stopping on a random page. A love spell. But the details were off. The caster had to wear something belonging to the victim's true mate. And the victim had to wear something carrying the caster's sigil.

But he didn't have a single thing of hers on him.

I needed answers. Fast. And I knew just who to ask. I shoved the book into a bag, flew down the stairs, and burst out the door. I ran until my lungs burned, hit the dirt in the clearing, opened my mind, and screamed into the void, "I need you!"

"What's the trouble, child?" I opened my eyes. My great-grandmother was sitting right there in the clearing. "Something is wrong..."
