Chapter 459

The blade went deeper. God, the burn was back. I didn't hold it in this time. I sucked in air and just let loose. A total, throat-shredding shriek. I screamed out the agony, screamed out the fury. I cursed the Moon Goddess, cursed my parents, cursed my dead ancestors. I dropped so many F-bombs the nurse actually snickered.

Then came the waterworks. Why did crying feel so damn important right now? Beats me, but I couldn't stop them. It wasn't even about the physical pain. Maybe it was just a dam breaking. Flushing out years of pure, unadulterated bullshit. Every drop of tears carried a gallon of pent-up rage. All that hate I'd bottled up for my old man, for Orion.

For my own stupid self.

It all drained out. By the time Rhys stepped back, I was a broken, bloody mess—but my head was totally clear. I knew exactly what my next move had to be.

"All set," Rhys said.

I blinked, frowning. "Wait, what about my ribs?"

Rhys let out a chuckle. "Popped back in. Stitched up, too. Just gotta wrap you up, and you're good to go." He looked over at the nurse. "Hey, I'm gonna wash my hands and grab some bandages. Clean her up, will ya?" He glanced back at me. "I'll snag you a clean shirt."

"Thanks," I muttered. He hauled me up a bit, and the nurse hit the bed controls to sit me upright.

"No sweat." He nodded toward my purse. "Your phone's been blowing up with calls and texts while you were passed out and screaming. You've got a minute to check 'em while Jenn grabs the cleaning supplies."

I gave him a nod. Jenn leaned over, whispering, "You were a badass." I forced a smile, slumping against the mattress to catch my breath. After a beat, I fished my phone out. Missed calls everywhere. But seeing Orion's name on the screen made me freeze. God knows why. The guy hadn't called me in forever.

Umbra crept out from the tree line in my head. She looked pissed as hell, but hey, at least she wasn't yelling.

Terra trailed right behind her. *Probably because this is the first time you've dealt with him since reading those letters,* Terra chimed in.

I rubbed my eyes, hard. *Don't even talk to me about those letters right now.* I dreaded making this call, but I absolutely needed his green light. I stared at the screen, stalling. Then, my laptop dinged. Perfect excuse to procrastinate. I dragged it out and flipped it open.

*Cipher: I've been doing some thinking. We seriously need to meet up face-to-face.*

My stomach did a flip. Man, I wanted to. I really did. But I was completely out of time.

*Cipher: You there?*

*Cipher: For fuck's sake, Lyra... why are you ghosting me now?*

Next up were texts from my dad.

*Deadmanwalking: Yo, got the King's okay. Just need a solid cover story to pull you out with the intel.*

*Deadmanwalking: Where the hell are you? Orion got a tip from some pack doc who swore he heard you screaming in the clinic. Did you get hurt? What's going on?*

*Deadmanwalking: Answer me, baby girl, or I'm calling right now.*

The sheer panic in his texts made me wince. Somewhere in the background, the clinic's landline started ringing. My phone buzzed in my hand again. I slammed the laptop shut. Screw it, they could wait. But then my mom's name flashed on the screen. I swiped to answer.

"Lyra! What in the actual fuck is happening? Why are you dodging my calls?" She was trying not to lose her mind.

I swallowed the lump in my throat. "Damon jumped me."

A sharp gasp on the other end. "I'm on my way."

"Mom, wait—"

I heard her yell to someone else. "Juniper! Damon went after Lyra. I'm heading to the pack right now!"

"Say what?!" Juniper, Daisy, and Luna all shrieked in the background. I winced. Oh, great.

"Mom!" I yelled into the phone.

"What?!" she barked, the sound of her heavy footsteps starting up.

"You can't come," I said. She grunted. "Listen to me! Do not show up here. Number one, you're on the other side of the damn country." I lowered my voice, hinting that we had ears around us. "Number two, the surgery's already over."

"Surgery?!" she screamed.

"And finally, I've got to pull this whole thing off tonight. I've got a dinner to attend."

She was out of breath now. "Are you freaking brain-dead?" I let out a dry laugh. "No, seriously, Lyra. Did I raise an idiot? You honestly think I'm gonna sit tight across the country when my kid gets hurt?"

I shook my head, even though she couldn't see me. "I'm not stupid, Mom. I'm a grown-ass woman, and I've got this handled. I am not running away from this pack with my mommy holding my hand like some whipped dog. When I leave this place, I'm walking out head high, spine straight, with a giant 'kiss my ass' sign on my back."
