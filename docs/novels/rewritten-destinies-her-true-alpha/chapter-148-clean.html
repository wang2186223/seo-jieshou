Chapter 148

I rolled my eyes, spun away from the door, and flopped onto the mattress. "Wait, what did that idiot just say?" Orion's voice buzzed through the speaker again.

"Oh, you know. The usual. He wants me back. Said screwing my sister was a massive mistake. Real Romeo, right?" I kicked off my shoes, trying to shake off the annoyance, and got back to business. "Look, forget him. I figured out why the hits are getting heavier."

"Yeah, we're definitely circling back to lover boy later," Orion grunted. A creak came through the line as he sank back into his seat. Dude must've been pacing. "But hit me. Why?"

"The shaman and his kid—she just got her powers—they ran," I muttered, keeping my voice down. My magic was still humming under my skin, keeping us safe, but I still didn't want to risk speaking up. "They're at my dad's place."

On his end, wood groaned hard under his weight. "Tell me you're joking."

"Man, I wish."

"Since when does the shaman have a kid?"

"She's five," I said, chewing on my lip.

"No way. That's too young." He sounded completely thrown.

"Well, she had her first vision right in my arms before I bailed on Magnus. I packed them off to my grandma's cliffside cabin. There's a magic bridge under the gazebo that leads straight to the pack lands." Why was I spilling my guts to this guy? We were basically strangers.

*He's our king, duh,* Umbra chimed in, totally casual.

*He's gonna help us keep her safe,* Terra added, her voice quiet but dead serious. Even she trusted this guy.

"Why spill all this to me?" Orion asked, his tone dropping to a low murmur.

"Because my best friend called. Dad's hurt bad. Before he went under, he managed one word: gazebo. She had no clue what it meant, but she knew I would. The bastards behind this know the kid is there. I can't do this alone, Orion. I need you."

"Why trust me?"

"Because you're literally the only one I can. Plus, I'm stuck at school. I've got way too much shit to learn here."

"So drop out. Come back next year, or when the dust settles."

"No can do. My dad needs me to—"

"To do what, exactly?" Orion shifted, his chair letting out a sharp squeak. "Spill the plan. I'm in. Whatever you need."

I paused. My gut said *trust him*, so I went with it. "Dad told me to learn how to hack on my way back. He thinks Magnus is pulling the strings now—and was back when your old man got killed." Orion sucked in a sharp breath. "He's not totally sure, which is why he kept his mouth shut around you, but he's convinced Magnus is the big fish. Dad tried cracking Oakenfang's servers, but their external firewall is a beast. But the internal stuff..."

"He figures you can get inside because you're already past their outer defenses," Orion finished for me.

"Bingo." I let out a heavy sigh. "And I can get even closer if I play Damon. I've been dodging him, but if I let him play the hunter, I can reel him in and get right next to the system."

A low, nasty growl rattled my speaker. "Hell no."

"Come on, Orion, you know it's a solid plan."

"I said no. End of story."

"Fine, whatever. That's way down the road anyway. Right now, I'm just learning the baby steps in class."

"Is that why you signed up for computer science?"

"Yeah. Figure out the code, learn how they build the walls, then figure out how to tear them down myself." Orion let out a low chuckle.

"Damn, you're smart."

"Thanks."

"But it's not gonna work."

"Wait, what?"

"I mean, it'll take way too long." Keyboard clacking started up on his end. "Look, I'm on campus Mondays and Fridays. After class, we can hang for an hour and I'll teach you the real shit."

"Wait, you know how to hack?"

I could practically hear him grinning. "Yeah. Told you, it's my thing." More clacking. "I break into human networks to make some extra cash for the council. Totally legal, but it's basically free money and a ton of fun."

"So... you're gonna teach me?"

"Yep. And I've got your back." He muffled the phone. "Lorna, get my beta, gamma, and second gamma in here. Now."

"Wait, you have a pack?"

"Kind of." He dodged it, and I wasn't about to push. Not my business anyway. A door creaked open on his end. "I need a squad sent to Silvermoon immediately. Keep it completely hush-hush. No other packs can get wind of this."

"Sure, but what's the play?" a quiet voice asked near his phone. Instantly, Umbra's mental fur stood on end.

"The shaman and his kid are holed up there," Orion ordered. "They need eyes on them. Keep them safe."
