Chapter 101

I glanced from Mom to Ronnie. "Yep. Sure is."

"You unpacked yet? Started on anything?" my dad's voice crackled through the phone.

I snorted. "Dad, I've been back for like, five minutes. Literally just scrubbing my room."

"Getting rid of that old stink, huh?"

"You bet. Oh, and the Alpha and his kid cornered me the second I stepped foot in the house."

Dad let out a dry laugh. "Unbelievable. Bastards tried to ambush you right off the bat." His tone flipped, turning dead serious. "Ronnie, listen up. I'm getting everything set up on my end."

"Hey, Dad?" I chimed in.

"Yeah, kiddo?"

"How quick can we get that plane back here?"

"Hold on. We might get lucky." I heard a clack as he tossed his cell on the desk and grabbed his landline. "Finnian! Where the hell are you?"

A beat of silence.

"How long till you guys reach the bird?" Dad muttered a few "uh-huhs." Then, he said the words that finally stopped my stomach from doing backflips. "Change of plans. Stay put at the tarmac. Lyra's got two people heading your way. Remember Caspian? Yeah, him and his kid. It's urgent. If they're running late, just wait out there for them. Appreciate it, man." He slammed the receiver down and grabbed his cell again. "You catch all that, sweetie?"

"Loud and clear, Dad." I shot Ronnie a look. He gave me a firm nod.

"Thanks, Caspian," Ronnie called out, leaning down to plant a kiss on Luna's forehead.

"Don't sweat it. Catch you later, kid," Dad said. He was about to hang up, then paused. "Hey... how's your mother?"

"She's fine." I glanced at Mom. Her head snapped toward the phone, totally caught off guard.

"God, Celestia... I miss her so much," Dad murmured, his voice dropping to a whisper. "I love you both. Don't ever forget that."

"I know, Dad. Love you too." I cut the call before I could catch Mom's eye. I spun around to face the two shamans. "You guys need to haul ass. Now."

"Wait, what?" Ronnie blinked.

"You set off Magnus's radar. I'd bet my life on it. If that psycho thinks you're keeping secrets, he won't stop. He'll hunt you down like a rabid dog."

Mom chimed in, nodding fast. "She's not wrong. You made a massive scene when Damon came back to say goodbye to Lyra." She locked eyes with me. "If I had to guess, Magnus is already putting together a hunting party."

"Forget your house," I snapped. "You're already past the pack borders. You've got a head start. Run straight to the airport and find Finnian." I shoved my phone into my pocket. "Here, take my number. If shit hits the fan, call me and I'll get ahold of Finnian." I spat out the digits.

"But our stuff—our clothes—" Ronnie started, but Luna's tiny hand slapped gently against his cheek, cutting him off.

"No going back, Daddy. They're already at the house."

We all froze, staring down at the little girl. Her eyes had gone completely milky white again.

"They're right," Luna whispered. "He's hunting us. He thinks you've got a pregnant mate hidden away. He's dying to grab a shaman's kid. But he's clueless. The kid is already right here. The firstborn of a nine-hundred-year-old shaman. A power nobody has ever seen." She whipped her blank, white eyes toward me. "Three-spirited shifter. You're next on the hit list. Stick to the road your dad laid out, but watch your step. One wrong move and we're all dead. The shaman kid isn't the only wildcard in this war."

Mom gasped, her face draining of color. "War?"

We both looked at her. Luna, still buzzing with raw, creepy power, stared right back. "You're clueless about so much, Luna," she said, addressing Mom. Then she looked back at me. "But you'll lay it all out when the time is right. They have to get back together."

I gave a quick nod. Instantly, the white cleared from Luna's eyes. A cold dread slammed into my stomach. Something bad was coming. "Move it," I urged. "Get out now, or you're trapped."

Ronnie didn't waste another second. He bolted out the back door, leaving the shop wide open.

"Come on, sweetie. Let's clear out too," Mom said. I nodded, and we headed toward the front. Mom shot me a sideways look, trying to act casual but failing to hide her smirk. "Does your dad always talk to you like that?"

"Every single time," I said, playing totally dumb. "He just likes making sure I know he loves and misses me."

Mom scowled, shoving the back door open. I grabbed a spray bottle off the rack as we passed, and we stepped outside. "We locking up?"

Mom turned around, jingling her keys with a smirk. "Got the spare right here." She clicked the lock. "Alright, what's next?"

"Pizza," I said, as she headed for the car.

"Right. Pizza."

Man, it felt like a lifetime ago since we ordered that food. In just a few crazy minutes, my whole damn world had flipped upside down.
