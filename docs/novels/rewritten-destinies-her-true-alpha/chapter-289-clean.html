NovelVibe

Chapter 289

"How the hell do you know all this?"

"Told ya," Luna said, giving Juniper a quick nod. "Japanese packs don't shut up about their bloodlines. We all knew Amaterasu’s main family. But seriously, how does Juniper know?"

Fawn jabbed a finger at me. "Yeah, and what about Lyra? How's she know?"

I just flashed a smirk.

Luna whipped her head toward Juniper. "Spill it. How do you know about Ma'at's bloodline?"

Juniper leaned back, eyes locked on the moon. "Easy. She built my family from scratch. We worship the Moon Celestia and Ma'at—the big boss of the stars. Ma'at heard the wolves howling, loved the sound, and wanted that music in Egypt. So she begged the Moon Celestia for the green light to start our line. My dad? He's got her raw power in his veins."

Fawn’s face fell. "Are you freaking kidding me? That's why you're so damn fast? No wonder I couldn't beat you in a race."

We all cracked up, remembering Fawn eating Juniper's dust for months.

The laughter died. One by one, their eyes burned into me. I ignored them, shoved my shovel into the dirt, and kept filling the grave.

"I know because the first family comes from the Moon Celestia. Just like us Lycans," I said, dumping another shovel of dirt. "My line has her actual blood." I kept shoveling, spitting out the legend of the very first Lycan. How she spotted him dying on a mountain, trapped in the sky, and literally bled herself dry to keep him breathing. "My witch side? That's from the triple goddess." They nodded, tracking. "But my wolf? That comes from both of them."

Luna lunged forward. "Wait, what? How is that even possible? You sure about that?"

I threw the last pile of dirt on the grave, slammed the shovel down, and looked them dead in the eye. "I saw it. With my own eyes."

"A vision?" Daisy whispered.

I nodded. "Way back, before concrete took over, a witch and her kid lived in these woods. She was a descendant of the triple goddess, teaching the boy her craft. Then humans moved in. They found the local wolves and started slaughtering them. But the Moon Celestia loved those wolves. She'd walk with them, keeping them on high alert. The wolves spotted the witch's kid. They thought he was one of the hunters and tore him to shreds." I squeezed my eyes shut, the bloody vision flashing in my head. "The witch found her boy. Mangled, barely breathing. She screamed for the triple goddess to save him, but she couldn't do a damn thing. But the Moon Celestia heard her crying and came down. She realized her wolves screwed up. She offered a deal, and the witch took it instantly. She didn't care if her son turned into a monster, she just wanted him alive. Boom. That's how our kind started."

Fawn scrambled to her knees. "Holy crap. You're royalty. The original bloodline." I nodded. "And your dad..."

I nodded again.

Daisy bit her lip, looking tense. "Is that why they went after him?"

I shrugged. "My dad kept a tight lid on it. Most of the pack has zero clue. And the ones who do? There's a magic gag order on them. They physically can't leak it to outsiders."

Juniper snorted, waving her hand at the group. "Uh, hello? What are we, chopped liver?"

"Hell no," I said. "You're my sisters. Besides, that gag order doesn't apply to me. I'm the one who's supposed to enforce the damn spell."

Luna threw her head back, groaning. "Okay, so I'm side-branch, but my family goes back to the Japanese originals." She pointed at Juniper. "You're Egyptian royalty." Then she glared at Daisy and Fawn. "What are the freaking odds you two aren't carrying some god's blood too?"

Total silence hit us. I stared up at the night sky, my mind racing. No way. Were we really a bunch of god-blooded freaks who just happened to become best friends?

*Are you seriously asking that?* Umbra snorted in my head. *The goddess said she wanted champions, dummy. You think she'd leave you to fight solo? She wants us to have backup.*

*She wants us to win, Lyra,* Terra chimed in, settling down right next to my magic. *And winning means having a Plan B.*

I took a deep breath, letting their words sink in. They were right. The goddesses wanted us alive. We were their kids, their anchors on this earth. When humans stopped believing, we stayed loyal. But still, this was too perfect.

"Hey," I muttered. "What if there's a way to test this?"
