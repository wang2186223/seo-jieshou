Chapter 269

I pushed through the media circus and grabbed my seat. Slid in next to the other grads, traded quick nods, and tried to get comfortable. Then this guy next to me turned around, looking like his dog just died.

"Hey. What they're pulling on you? It's total crap, Lyra." I blinked, staring hard at Chad. My eyebrow shot up. "Look," he muttered, "we aren't buddies. Just shared some classes. But I'm not blind. I know what you and your old man did for this place. Personally, you annoy the hell out of me. But your family? You guys don't deserve this garbage." He shook his head, scowling. "The whole class talked. We're on the same page. They're putting a bullseye on your back, and timing it for graduation is no accident. Just wanted to give you a heads-up. We've got your back, alright?"

I just gaped at him. Was this a joke? The guy basically detested me since day one. He must've read the sheer disbelief on my face, because a faint smirk cracked his lips. "Told you, I still don't like you. But you saved my family. And me. We're not going to sit here and watch you get thrown to the wolves." His eyes locked onto mine. Dead serious. But wait—did he say *we*?

"Everyone?" I muttered, doubting it. But then I scanned the room. My department mates were all staring right at me. Then, kids from the other majors turned around. One by one, they bowed their heads. No joke—practically the whole damn graduating class was nodding at me. Tears stung my eyes. I had to look away, fast. When I glanced back at Chad, he gave me a firm nod. He opened his mouth to speak, but Magnus strutted onto the stage and cut him off.

The guy grabbed the mic and scanned the crowd. His eyes locked onto me, practically dripping with smug satisfaction. He thought he'd won. Standing up there, puffing his chest out like some prize-winning show dog, wearing this greasy grin. He was riding high. Let him enjoy it. We'd see who got the last laugh when the dust settled.

"Alright, folks, quiet down," Magnus boomed into the mic. "We're getting started. Just a heads-up: after the speeches but before we hand out diplomas, we've got a surprise guest speaker and some off-program announcements. Bear with us." He flashed a slimy grin, stepped back, and waved a teacher up to kick things off.

We sat through the endless drone of speeches. Faculty went first, then the department heads. Finally, it was time for the big one—the valedictorian. The Art department head stepped up to the podium.

"Alright, everyone, stand up and help me welcome our valedictorian," the teacher announced. "This student didn't just ace every single class. She spent the last few years busting her butt for everyone here. She backed student groups, volunteered to sort out faculty schedules, and proved she's 100% dedicated to this school—and to our pack. She's the definition of trust, grit, and straight-up honesty."

Talk about a slap in the face. I felt a rush of pure satisfaction. Right on cue, Magnus's smug face started to twitch. He was totally lost. He had zero clue who the teacher was talking about. But I knew. "So, without further ado... let's hear it for our valedictorian: Seraphina Thorne!"

The crowd went wild. Magnus's jaw hit the floor. Guess the staff kept him completely in the dark on that little detail. He glared at someone behind me. I glanced back. Damon was frowning, totally baffled. He leaned in close. "Wait. *You're* the valedictorian?"

"Yep," I whispered back, grinning. "Found out last month when finals wrapped." I stood up, smoothing my gown, and marched up to the stage. In the back, the press cameras were rolling, catching every second. I gripped the podium. "Hey there, class of 2021." I looked out at the sea of faces. "Don't worry, I'll keep this short. You've already sat through a million speeches way fancier than whatever I wrote." That got a few laughs. "Look, I had a whole speech ready to go. But some crap went down today, and I think we need to talk about it."

Behind me, I heard Magnus scrape his chair back, but I didn't even blink. "Everyone in this room—the students, the teachers—you all know what really happened these past few years. But the outside world doesn't. So yeah, I get why today's circus is happening. But I'm not letting them get away with it."

"Lyra, shut it. This isn't the place," Magnus hissed, lunging toward me.

I let out a low, vicious growl. He froze instantly, his hand dangling inches from my mic.
