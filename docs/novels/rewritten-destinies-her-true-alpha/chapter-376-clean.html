Chapter 376

Watching Lyra Bloom cuddle that wolf absolutely gutted me. I leaned in close, trying to catch her eye. "Faelan?" I muttered.

Lyra nodded, her cheek pressed against the pup's mangy, lifeless fur. "Yeah. The second I touched her, boom, I just knew her name. Don't ask me how." She looked up. "She's sick too, right? Just like me?"

I swallowed hard and nodded. "Yeah, kiddo. She is." Lyra squeezed the pup tighter.

"But why is she so tiny?" Lyra squinted one eye open, clutching the runt. "Aren't wolves supposed to be huge? And shouldn't we have connected way before this?"

I glanced at Umbra. Hell, I’d been talking to my wolf since I was five. She was my rock, my ride-or-die. Looking at these two just made my chest ache. "Yeah, she should be bigger," I said, my voice cracking. "But so should you. This damn sickness drained your power, starved your strength, and just... stopped you both from growing."

Her eyes went totally hollow. "It's fine, honestly."

"What's fine?"

"That I'm gonna die."

She shut her eyes. The pup whimpered, rolling over in her arms. Faelan forced one eyelid open, using every ounce of juice she had left to lick Lyra's cheek. Even half-dead, the wolf was fighting to comfort her human. Lyra's lips trembled as she kissed the pup's tiny nose. "I've had a whole year to accept it," Lyra said, turning to face me. "Accept that I'll never grow up. That I have to leave Mom and Dad. On my worst days, when I couldn't even get out of bed, I wrote them letters. Just drawings at first. But once I learned to spell, I wrote down exactly how much I love them."

"Oh, Lyra..." I brushed a hand through her hair. Instantly, my magic surged. It shot straight through her, and I felt her spark flare up. Damn, my healing power was hitting way harder than I expected.

Umbra felt the jolt too and locked eyes with me. "Pick up the pup," she urged. I looked down at Faelan, and those tiny eyes fluttered open again.

The pup couldn't talk, but her eyes screamed pure agony, terror, and isolation. "Come here, girl," I whispered, scooping Faelan up. Boom—my magic dumped straight into her tiny body. Holy hell. She was literally just skin and bones. But right then, the whole picture clicked in my head.

"What? What is it?" Lyra scrambled up, propping herself against Umbra's side.

Umbra curled around her, keeping her steady while watching me hold the pup. "What did they teach you about the bond?" Umbra asked.

"That your wolf is your ultimate best friend. You share one body, one soul. That's the school version, anyway." Lyra looked at her.

Umbra nodded. "True, but it's way deeper than that. We're your ride-or-die, your secret-keeper, and... your shield."

Lyra frowned, totally confused. "What do you mean, shield?"

"It means," I interrupted, "that when Faelan woke up inside your head, you were already dying. She didn't hesitate. She threw herself in front of the bullet to keep you breathing."

Lyra's head whipped toward the pup in my hands. She dragged herself over to me, her lower lip shaking. "But... she didn't even know me. We never spoke. Is she dying because of me?"

I shoved another wave of healing magic into the pup, targeting her failing lungs first. Screw her patchy fur; we needed her breathing. "Not because of you, kid," I said. "For you."

Lyra buried her face in her hands, sobbing uncontrollably. "Why? Why would she do that for me?"

Umbra pressed closer, licking a tear off Lyra's cheek. "When we first wake up, we just sit quietly in your head and watch the world through your eyes. Who you are shapes who we become. You're sweet and kind, so she became sweet and kind. But honestly, it's even simpler. She's your protector. The second she opened her eyes, her only thought was you. She felt you dying, felt your agony, and swallowed it all just to buy you some time."

Lyra dropped her hands, staring at me through wet eyes. "Time for what?" she sobbed, gently stroking Faelan's back. "I'm not living without her. I won't."

"You might survive without your wolf, technically," I said, running a finger down Faelan's snout. "But she didn't know that. She just wanted to take the hit so you'd have enough time to say goodbye."

Lyra shook her head, crying. "But I was always gonna die anyway."

"Yeah," I nodded. "But she bought you the time to accept it. To write those letters. To tell your parents you love them. And, hopefully, to get a shot."

"A shot at what?" Lyra pushed herself up on trembling legs. "A shot at watching my wolf die in front of me?"

"No, baby girl. A shot at a cure. A shot at meeting me."
