Chapter 1030

The back of the black Maybach was dead silent. Lyra stared at her phone, thumb flicking across the glass. Her face went totally rigid.

Julian felt the sudden freeze in the air and looked over. He slid his hand over hers, lacing their fingers. "Hey. What's up?" He rubbed his thumb over her knuckles, trying to take the edge off. "Still pissed about that scumbag? Come on. A loser like him doesn't deserve the rent space in your head."

"Him?" Lyra looked up. Her eyes were pure, flat ice. "Please. Like he's even worth a second of my time."

He wasn't, obviously. Caspian was a nobody. A zero.

She shoved the screen in front of his face. Rows of raw, decrypted code scrolled down. "Look at this."

It was the stuff she’d pulled off the chip from that creepy abandoned factory basement.

The list was a straight-up horror show. Names. Ages. Blood types. Intake dates. Health scores. Row after row of real people.

"The Doyles are way sicker than we thought," Lyra spat, her voice dropping to a freezing whisper. "I only read the first few pages. I figured they were just hunting poor, smart kids at Marington and those other fancy colleges." She paused, her jaw clenching hard. "But there are kids on here, Julian. Actual kids."

She practically vibrated with rage. Lyra never lost her cool like this. Never.

Julian grabbed the phone, his eyes scanning the next sheet. His pupils shrunk to pinpricks. His grip almost cracked the screen.

"Jesus. This one's eight," he muttered, his voice turning lethal. "And this one... and this one..." He stared at the lines. "They’re all from the middle of nowhere. Orphanages."

His face went completely deadpan, cold as a grave.

These were throwaway kids. Half of them weren't even in the system. No birth certificates, no IDs. The kind of kids who could just disappear, and nobody would ever call the cops.

He scrolled down. It got worse.

Experiment numbers. Drug dosages. Vitals. And next to almost every single name, one word: *Decommissioned.*

Julian’s eyes went black. These were living, breathing people.

"Sick bastards," he growled. The veins in his hand popped, his knuckles turning white. He wanted to kill someone. The Doyles were rotten straight to the bone.

Lyra put her hand over his white knuckles. Her face was grim. "We have to move faster," she said. "The Doyles are just the face of this. If we don't tear the whole damn tree out by the roots, more kids are going to die."

Julian turned his hand over, squeezing her cold fingers to warm them up. "I got you. We have the files now. We're going to burn them with it." He squeezed tighter. "I'll get the Blackwood dark web guys on this. We'll watch every single move the Doyles make. 24/7."

"Yeah," Lyra muttered. She leaned back against the leather seat and shut her eyes.

They needed to move now. Like, yesterday.

Those ID numbers. Those dates. The rows where there wasn't even a name, just a serial number. Some of these kids didn't even get to have a name.

...

Thirty minutes later, the Maybach rolled up the driveway of Lancaster Manor.

Lyra hopped out. Ethan and the rest of the crew piled out of the SUV behind them. They walked right into the rest of the Lancasters, who had just pulled up too.

Reginald was in his wheelchair, Eleanor standing right behind him.

"Grandpa? Mom? Dad? Ethan?" Lyra looked at the group, the pieces clicking in her head. "Wait, did you guys actually go to the competition?"
