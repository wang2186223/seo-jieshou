Chapter 575

Orion was right on my heels. Juniper and Gideon? Tailgating him. But I didn't give a damn. My whole world had shrunk to the pitch-black tunnel in front of my face. I just needed to see that light at the end of this damn hole.

"Lyra." Orion reached out. I grabbed his hand, locking our fingers together tight.

"He's trapped, Orion. The cave collapsed. He's dying." The panic I'd been shoving down exploded. My voice shook like crazy as I dragged him forward. "Look, I was in his head. I know how that sounds, totally nuts, but I *was* him. I felt the rocks crushing my chest. I heard the other wolves screaming under the rubble."

*Whack.* My foot clipped the bottom step, and my knee slammed hard into the next one. "Fuck!" I threw my hands out, barely saving my face from the stone stairs.

Orion hauled me up, and we kept climbing. "I believe you. I can hear the terror in your voice," he panted. "But is this past, present, or future? What are we dealing with here?"

That chilled me to the bone. I shut my eyes for a split second. "God, I wish it was a memory. At least that would mean he survived. But my gut is screaming that he's suffocating right now."

I shoved the hatch. It slammed open against the dirt. I summoned a wisp of light, illuminating the creepy, dark space under the gazebo. I scrambled, found the release panel, and *click*—I was out. I bolted past the house, legs pumping.

People spilled out of the back door. "Lyra?" Kaelen and Elara ran toward me, eyes wide. "Where the hell did you just come from?"

I waved them off, gasping for air. "Where's my dad? Where is he?"

Elara's face fell apart. "He's in the caves." My heart literally stopped. "Lyra, I don't think he made it. His radio's been dead for over an hour."

I squeezed Orion's hand until my knuckles turned white. He stepped in front of me, frowning. "Wait, why are you guys even at the house?"

Kaelen glanced at Elara. "Finnian sent us back to check for any word from you." He looked back at the dark windows. "I was literally looking for my phone charger when Elara looked out and saw you guys." He wiped sweat from his forehead.

"Where is the rest of the pack?" Juniper asked, looking around. "The place is a ghost town."

"Your dad and the fighters are hunting the rogues. The ones who blew the caves and ran," Kaelen said, nodding toward the dark woods. "We set up a base camp by the cave entrance. The Omegas are hauling food and medical gear back and forth."

"Why the hell do that?" Gideon pointed down the street toward the clinic. "Why not bring the wounded back here? It's way safer."

Elara looked at me, tears spilling over. "Because nobody is leaving the Alpha behind. Anyone who can hold a shovel is digging."

I spun around and let go. Umbra exploded out of me faster than ever. Black fur ripped across my skin. I leaped, hitting the dirt on all fours, and bolted. "Lyra, wait!" Elara screamed. "I can't shift!"

"Then bring the others. I'm not waiting," Umbra growled back, cold as ice. Shadow pulled up right beside me, matching my pace. "You followed me?"

"You think I'd let my mate go save our dad alone?" Shadow snorted. "Come on, babe. I'm not leaving your side." He glanced at me. "But listen."

I kept my eyes on the trail. "What?"

"Once we get your dad out, save the pack, and fix this whole damn world... you and me are going for a run. Just us." He flashed a wicked wolfish grin. "I seriously miss chasing you through these woods." His deep growl rattled the branches above us.

If my wolf could blush, she'd be bright red. *Focus!* I snapped at her. She pushed harder. "Focus, mate," she threw back at Shadow. "Let's save Dad first, then we can talk about that run." We wove through the thick pines, and I surged forward as the cliff came into view.

We burst through the brush into a wall of bright lights. The clearing was pure chaos. Wolves were sprinting around, arms loaded with gear. Screams echoed from the left. That's where they set up the medical tent.

It was a massive event tent, and it looked packed to the brim. The smell of blood hit my nose. Normally, I'd stop to help, but then a voice cut through the noise.

"Come on, Leo," Finnian choked out. "Talk to me, man."

I threw myself off the cliff, sliding and rolling down the steep dirt slope. Finnian was leaning against the collapsed cave entrance while wolves hauled heavy rocks away. He heard the noise, spun around, and saw me charging toward him. His eyes filled with pure agony. He stood up, opening his arms. "Pup," he whispered, lips shaking. He was terrified.

And in his eyes, I saw it. Death. My father was dying.
