Chapter 36

Sure, in their tax bracket, a college degree was basically a prop, but you still needed the paper. He couldn't stand the thought of some trust-fund snobs whispering behind Lyra’s back. It’d crush her. But hey, her life, her rules. If she wanted to go to school, he’d buy the damn university. If not? He’d ruin the first idiot who dared to smirk at her. Simple as that.

Lyra saw right through the cheap stunt. Guess Vivienne forgot the memo about staying in her lane. Lyra smirked, slowly tilting her chin up. Her eyes were dead, icy, and totally blank.

Vivienne froze, a cold sweat breaking out on her neck. "L-Lyra... look, I didn't mean it like that. I was just, you know..."

"Oh, totally," Lyra cut in, her voice dripping with lazy sarcasm. "You just wanted to flex your straight-A report card over my dropout status. Nice ego boost, huh?" She let out a dry laugh. "What’s the play here? Want me to bow down? Or should I just pack my bags and cry myself out the front door?"

Bullseye. The words ripped right through Vivienne's fake-nice act, exposing her dirty laundry. Her face went from ghost-white to bright red. She never expected Lyra to go nuclear right at the dinner table.

She scrambled out of her chair, hands waving. "No! No way! How can you say that? I’m literally just looking out for you, okay? For the family. I'm trying to help!"

"Pass." Lyra didn't even look at her. Her phone buzzed in her hand. She slid back her chair. "I'm heading out."

Eleanor jumped up. "Wait, it's pitch black outside. Where are you going? Let me come with."

"Just seeing a friend." Lyra fired off a quick text and shoved the phone into her jeans.

"Oh, good, you need to get out more," Eleanor beamed. "Take the driver. Or, like, grab any set of keys from the garage."

Frederick whipped out his wallet and tossed a black card onto the table. "Can't hang out without cash. Take this. Swipe whatever. We’ve got money to burn, kiddo. Do your thing."

That was a direct slap to Vivienne's little lecture. The Lancasters didn't need a piece of paper to prove their worth, and they sure as hell didn't need Lyra to have one. If she wanted to be a professional couch potato, they’d fund it.

Vivienne got the message loud and clear. Her face drained of color, her nails digging into her palms. Since day one, her dad had run a tight ship. She and her brothers had to jump through hoops. Every single win she had was paid for in sweat and tears. So why did the rules suddenly fly out the window for Lyra?

Jealousy burned hot in her chest. She glared at Lyra’s back as she walked away. A literal farm girl in the big city—who the hell could she even know in Vaelion?

"Meeting a friend"? Yeah, right. Total BS. Lyra was probably just running away because she couldn't face her trash grades and dropout status. And even if she did have "friends," they were probably some low-life losers.

Vivienne gritted her teeth, shoved her chair back, and tailed her.
