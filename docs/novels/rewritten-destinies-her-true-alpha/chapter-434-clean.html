Chapter 434

Her words hit me like a slap. Total BS, but she actually believed it.

"Oh, sweetie." I grabbed her cheeks. "Listen to me. Yeah, you're a shaman. You see creepy stuff. But that doesn't mean I'm gonna let this crap ruin your childhood. You're a kid, period. Stay that way."

She stuck her lip out. "But why?"

I dropped to my knees, staring straight into her eyes. "Because being a grown-up sucks. Big time. It's all bills, chores, and endless stress. You constantly freak out over what's next, what you messed up, and stuff you can't even fix. It's a total nightmare." I yanked her pigtails gently. "Just be a kid. Once this shitshow is over, you're going to a real school. You'll play, goof off, and grow up slow. No rushing, deal?"

She stared at me, processing, then gave a slow nod. "Deal."

I grinned, planted a kiss on her forehead, and muttered, "Good girl."

I stood up and bolted out. Luna scrambled after me, squeezing her tiny hand into mine as we headed down the stairs.

"Alright, I'll be back in a bit. Then we tackle that mountain of paperwork."

"Are we hunting for something?" she whispered, eyes wide with excitement.

"Honestly? No clue. Maybe?" I laughed at her scrunched-up face. "Guess we'll find out, kiddo." I threw her a wink and stopped right in front of Juniper and Daisy. "Yo."

"Hey," Juniper said, scrubbing a plate with a smile. "How're you holding up?"

Daisy walked over, rubbing my back. "Yeah, your mom spilled the beans. That's some heavy shit."

I let out a sharp breath. "Tell me about it." I shook my head to clear the fog. "But I've got bigger fish to fry." I leaned close to Daisy, lowering my voice. "When I get back, I'm hitting the paperwork with Luna. Once she's knocked out, we talk strategy."

Juniper's ears perked up. "Strategy? We making a move?"

I nodded. "Yep. But I need to talk to my old man first."

Daisy nodded. "Fair enough." She started to turn, then glanced back. "Where you heading now?"

"Back to the old apartment." I walked toward the door. "My dad... he used magic to lock me out of some secrets. Claimed it was 'for my own good'."

Juniper snorted. "Classic dad move. They think they're geniuses, but they're just terrified of their babies growing up."

I rolled my eyes. "Ugh, spot on." I laughed, heading for the back door. "Just a quick run there and back. Let my mom know where I went when she wakes up."

Juniper smirked. "Oh, she's been up. She's outside right now, texting your dad." She wiggled her eyebrows, making my stomach turn.

"Ugh, gag me."

The girls cracked up as I stepped outside. My mom was sitting there in a tee and shorts, glued to her screen. Seriously, she looked like a hot twenty-something, not a fifty-year-old. She was smirking at her screen, and I shuddered. "Mom, gross. Stop."

Her head whipped up. She flushed bright red, sliding the phone onto the table. "Oh! Sweetie. Didn't hear you come out." Her ears turned pink as she looked away.

I snickered. "Are you kidding me? You're a literal werewolf and you didn't hear me? Tell Dad I said hi." I shook my head, staring down at her.

Her ears turned tomato red. "Oh, shut up. He just... sent a funny text." She couldn't even look me in the eye.

I threw my hands up. "Whoa, TMI. I do not want the details." She laughed as I gave her shoulder a playful shove. Her face was glowing—I hadn't seen her like this in forever. "You look happy."

She froze for a second. Then her smile stretched even wider. "I am happy." She stared at her phone, tapping the screen. "Soon, I get my mate back. We get to live the life the Goddess promised us." She leaned back, locking eyes with me. "I wasted years being pissed off, thinking he betrayed us. But now? Realizing I was the one who screwed up, and he's still ready to take me back? It's a massive reality check."

"Meaning what?" I asked.

"Look, I'm not saying stay with a jerk or tolerate abuse. But let me tell you something. Forgiving someone—or even just forgiving yourself—is way better than drowning in anger and pain." Her eyes softened. "Even if you and Orion are completely done, forgiving him... and yourself... costs zero dollars. And it'll save you a hell of a lot of wasted time."
