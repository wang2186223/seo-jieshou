Chapter 96

The cameras tracked him. Ian hugged his laptop, ducking into a tiny, windowless bunker of a room. Boom. The video feed went fuzzy with static the second he crossed the threshold.

"Smart kid," Mr. Harrison muttered. His smile was pure ice. "He knows exactly which corner blocks the bugs and the lenses."

Man, it stung. Harrison had hand-picked this kid. Who expects their own protégé to stab them in the back? Lyra had warned him, too. *Watch him,* she'd said. What a waste. The kid had serious chops, but his morals were trash. Harrison had poured years into mentoring him, only to breed a snake.

Sixty seconds of static, then—*pop*—the screen cleared right up. Crystal clear.

The tech guy next to Harrison let out a low whistle. "Damn, Miss Lancaster is good. She knew he’d try to jam us. She pre-loaded a bypass script right onto that laptop."

That laptop was a trap. Juicy bait for a massive shark.

On screen, they watched Ian plug a black box into the port, trying to force his way in. Minutes ticked by. Sweat beaded on Ian's forehead. His face went tight, his hands shaking. He threw everything he had at it—every exploit, every line of code he knew.

Nothing. The system was a brick wall.

"What the hell?" Ian hissed, wiping his wet brow. "What kind of crazy-ass encryption is this? Come on!" His shirt was soaked. He was losing his mind, and the panic made him sloppy. The screen just mocked him: *ACCESS DENIED. ILLEGAL INTRUSION.* Red text flashing over and over.

Then, the laptop shrieked. A high-pitched beep. A new alert flashed: *TRACKING PROGRAM ACTIVATED.*

Ian froze. The red glow hit his face, draining every drop of color from his skin. Cold terror hit him like a truck. It clicked. Lyra wasn't just some smart girl. She was a monster. She was probably lightyears ahead of Harrison himself.

His face went gray. His chest thudded like a bass drum. Staring at the flashing red warning, his shaky fingers flew to his own keyboard. He booted up a dark-web chat client and pinged his contact.

Back in the control room, Harrison watched the kid panic and reach out to his handler. Harrison’s fists balled up, knuckles white. Watching your favorite student play spy for the enemy? It was a hard pill to swallow.

"He's spooked," Lyra said, her eyes turning to ice. "He'll run to his boss for cover and try to hand over whatever he got to save his skin. And they won't resist the 'Project Aegis' files. It's too juicy."

Right on cue, the chat popped up. Ian was talking to a user named "Siphon." The system grabbed the IP address instantly.

Lyra tapped the keyboard. "We got 'em. Lock down all frequencies. Let's bag them."

Julian leaned back, a lazy smirk on his face. "We've been tracking Siphon's East Asian cell forever. Thanks to you, we're pulling this weed out by the roots." He grabbed his radio, his lazy vibe vanishing instantly. "Target is active. Close the net. Catch them in the act."

Harrison watched them. They stood shoulder-to-shoulder, barely talking, but totally in sync. Like they could read each other's minds. He rubbed his gray hair and let out a breath. It was over. With Lyra running the show, the bad guys didn't stand a chance. He smiled to himself. *Man, to be young again.*

The feds moved fast. Using the coordinates from the Elyndor Institute, SWAT kicked the door off its hinges. They tackled Ian before he could even blink. Caught him dead to rights.

At the same time, the feds traced the "Siphon" link. A quiet, brutal sweep went down across Vaelion City. They bagged three Norvane spies and Siphon's top guys. Then, using Lyra's custom virus, they wiped every single byte of stolen Aegis data from the enemy servers.

Five days flew by.

For Vivienne, it was pure hell. She hadn't heard a peep from Ian since she handed him Lyra's laptop. First, he ignored her calls. Then, his phone went straight to voicemail.

She wanted dirt on Lyra, but this radio silence was driving her crazy. Where was the laptop? What was he doing with it? She drove down to D University to sniff around. Nothing. No one had seen Ian in five days. Exactly five days. The day she gave him the computer.

Her stomach did flips. Panic set in. Should she confess to Lyra? What if Ian screwed up and Lyra already knew? What if the family found out? She'd be ruined.

She went home, her mind spinning, totally paralyzed by what to do next.
